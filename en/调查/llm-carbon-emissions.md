# LLM Energy and Infrastructure Labor: Stop Treating “Carbon per Page” as a Universal Fact

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** The old article reduced AI energy use to catchy per-query or per-page carbon numbers. That is too fragile for archival work. A more defensible frame is data-center electricity demand, AI-accelerator growth, power and cooling infrastructure, and the labor required to build and operate it.

## Put the scale first

The IEA estimates that data centers consumed about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity use. In its 2025 base case, data-center demand rises to about 945 TWh by 2030.[^1]

Data centers are not synonymous with AI. Cloud computing, storage and conventional internet services also consume electricity. But the IEA identifies AI-driven accelerated servers as a major source of future growth.[^1]

A 2026 IEA update reported that global data-center electricity use rose about 17% in 2025, while electricity use in AI-focused data centers grew faster, by about 50%.[^2]

## Why “energy per query” is unstable

An AI request can be a short text completion, long-context reasoning, a multi-step agent, image generation or video generation. The IEA notes that simple text tasks have become much more efficient, while reasoning, video and agentic tasks can consume hundreds or thousands of times more energy per request.[^3]

A single Wh-per-query estimate from one model and hardware configuration should therefore not be generalized to the whole industry.

## The labor question

AI infrastructure requires construction workers, electricians, cooling and network engineers, chip and server supply-chain workers, grid operators and energy-project staff. It also affects utilities and communities where large new loads are built.

AI is often described as software replacing office labor. Yet its physical expansion is simultaneously creating an enormous capital-intensive infrastructure buildout.

## Electricity mix is not a slogan

IEA analysis shows data centers are supplied by a mix of renewables, gas, coal and nuclear power, with major regional differences.[^4] Renewable procurement contracts are not the same thing as the physical electricity mix in every hour.

## Evidence boundaries

- Data-center electricity is not all AI electricity.
- There is no permanent universal energy figure for “one ChatGPT query.”
- Contracted clean energy is not identical to physical supply.
- Aggregate electricity growth does not prove harm to a particular community without local evidence.
- The old “Llama-3-70B carbon per page versus a human” comparison is removed as a general claim.
