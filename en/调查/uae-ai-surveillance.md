# HOLD: UAE AI Surveillance

> **Status: HOLD (2026-08-28)**

The old article merged urban surveillance, facial recognition and worker monitoring into a single confident story about “AI labor surveillance” in the United Arab Emirates. Under the v2 evidence standard, that chain is not strong enough, so this entry is suspended as a formal investigation.

## What can be supported

Human Rights Watch reported in 2026 that migrant workers across Gulf states, including the UAE, faced increased surveillance of communications, warnings and pressure to self-censor discussion of working conditions during regional conflict. HRW interviewed 38 Indian, Nepali and Bangladeshi migrant workers based across Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE.[^1]

That supports a labor-rights issue involving surveillance and freedom to discuss working conditions.

## What cannot yet be supported

Current high-quality evidence is insufficient to establish that:

- UAE city-scale AI camera systems are systematically used to measure ordinary migrant workers' workplace performance;
- a headline camera count can be directly equated with labor surveillance;
- urban security, immigration control and employer workplace monitoring form one unified system;
- AI surveillance has produced a quantified wage, dismissal or productivity effect.

These are separate institutional layers and should not be collapsed into one narrative.

## What would remove the HOLD

The archive would need both:

1. concrete procurement, technical, contractual or investigative evidence that AI/biometric systems are used for worker identity, attendance, performance or movement monitoring; and
2. labor-outcome evidence such as worker testimony, litigation, regulatory records or systematic research linking those systems to pay, discipline, dismissal, grievance or organizing rights.

Until then, the archive retains Gulf migrant-worker communication surveillance as related context but does not present a proven “UAE AI labor surveillance” case.
