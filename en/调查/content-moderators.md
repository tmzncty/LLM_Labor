# Content Moderators: High-Risk Labor Behind Platform Safety

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** Content moderation predates generative AI, but it now overlaps directly with AI labor: human moderators protect users while also producing judgments that can train and evaluate automated safety systems. Evidence strongly supports high psychological risk and persistent outsourcing, but prevalence figures must distinguish research samples from litigation evidence.

## The work

Moderators decide whether posts, images and videos violate platform rules. They may encounter violence, suicide, self-harm, sexual abuse, hate speech and terrorism. Automated systems can filter some material, but ambiguous cases, local languages, appeals and high-risk judgments continue to require people.

Human decisions can also become training and evaluation data for automated moderation. Moderators are therefore both operations workers and, increasingly, safety-model trainers.

## Kenya and the courts

Meta used contractor Sama to run a Facebook moderation hub in Nairobi. When Sama exited content moderation in 2023, roughly 260 moderators were affected as Meta shifted work toward Majorel. Former moderators allege unfair dismissal, union-busting and exclusion from equivalent jobs at the successor contractor.

In September 2024 Reuters reported that Kenya's Court of Appeal allowed litigation involving Meta, Sama and the moderators to proceed in Kenya.[^1] Allowing a case to proceed is not a final finding of liability.

## Mental-health evidence

In December 2024 The Guardian reported medical assessments filed in Kenyan litigation. All 144 examined former moderators in that group were diagnosed with PTSD, generalized anxiety disorder and major depressive disorder, with many severe symptoms persisting after they left the job.[^2]

This is powerful evidence, but **not a random industry prevalence estimate**. The correct statement is “144 examined plaintiffs/former moderators in the litigation,” not “100% of content moderators develop PTSD.”

Public testimony from former moderator Sonia Kgomo also describes second-by-second productivity metrics, continuous exposure to extreme content and inadequate support.[^3]

## Outsourcing moves

In 2025 The Guardian and the Bureau of Investigative Journalism reported new disputes involving Meta-contracted moderation in Ghana. Workers alleged psychological harm, low pay, intense monitoring and retaliation; Meta said its contracts require fair compensation and support.[^4] The allegations remain subject to legal and factual dispute.

The broader pattern is clear enough to investigate: changing vendors or countries does not itself remove the labor risk.

## Why this belongs in AI labor history

Generative AI does not eliminate moderation. It creates new synthetic content, new false positives and new appeal/review work. Human judgments also help train safety classifiers. Automation may therefore push humans toward rarer, harder and more disturbing edge cases rather than remove them.

## Evidence boundaries

- Litigation medical samples are not industry-wide prevalence.
- Plaintiff allegations are not final judicial findings.
- Most moderators are contractors, not direct Meta employees.
- Automated moderation does not imply human moderation is disappearing.
