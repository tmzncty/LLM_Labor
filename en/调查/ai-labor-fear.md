# AI and Labor Fear

> Between 2025 and 2026, a panic about AI eliminating human jobs swept American society. Silicon Valley CEOs publicly predicted a "permanent underclass," 64% of Americans believed AI would reduce job opportunities, and young members of Generation Z began stockpiling skills like doomsday preppers preparing for unemployment. But the strangeness of this fear lies in this: those制造恐惧 are the same people制造 AI, and the solutions they propose have几乎为零 implementation mechanisms. The fear is real, but who利用恐惧 and whom it serves is a more important question than fear itself.

## I. Background

After ChatGPT's release in late 2022, "AI will replace human jobs" moved from internal discussions in tech circles to public discourse. But the real turning point came in 2025.[^1]

Three things happened that year. First, AI began truly entering corporate operations—not as demonstrations, but as reasons for layoffs. Block CEO Jack Dorsey cut nearly half his company's workforce in March 2026, citing AI编程助手 that "presented options to fundamentally change any company's structure."[^2] Meta laid off 8,000 employees in May 2026, with workers signing petitions反对 being tracked by AI systems.[^3]

Second, AI companies' revenue exploded. Anthropic's annualized revenue surged from $9 billion at the end of 2025 to $30 billion in 2026.[^4] AI-related investment accounted for 39% of US economic growth in the first three quarters of 2025.[^5]

Third, polls revealed a分裂的图景: 64% of Americans believed AI would reduce job opportunities (Pew 2025),[^6] 70% held this view (Quinnipiac 2025),[^7] 79% worried the government had no plan to protect workers, and 72% worried AI would压低 wages.[^8] Only 17% thought AI would have a positive impact.[^9]

AI was surging forward in economic indicators while resembling an approaching disaster in public opinion metrics.

## II. Core Facts

### Silicon Valley's Consensus

In April 2026, The New York Times published Jasmine Sun's (孙茉莉) article "Silicon Valley Is Bracing for a Permanent Underclass." She interviewed various Silicon Valley figures—engineers, venture capitalists, founders, doomers, accelerationists—and concluded: **their views on AI's impact on workers' prospects were remarkably一致, and all pessimistic.**[^1]

"Permanent underclass" describes a future where, after AI fully replaces human labor, class stratification becomes彻底固化—the rich own superintelligent machines, everyone else is unemployed and lives on welfare. This term is频繁使用 at Silicon Valley dinner parties and investment meetings.[^1]

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei (达里奥·阿莫德伊) predicted 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs might disappear by 2030.[^10] He raised the issue to the level of democracy:

> "The balance of power in democracy is built on ordinary people gaining leverage through creating economic value. If that doesn't exist, I think things get a little scary."[^10]

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman (山姆·奥特曼) predicted in 2021 that AI systems would be "unstoppable" and able to do any job humans can do.[^1] Palantir CEO Alex Karp (亚历克斯·卡普) said: "The biggest challenge AI faces in this country is political upheaval... When the country explodes, none of us make money."[^1] Karp's concern wasn't workers suffering but profits受损. The underlying logic of fear is: workers might rebel.

### Cross-Class Anxiety

AI anxiety crosses class lines. Blue-collar workers worry about being replaced by robots; white-collar workers worry their work will become like blue-collar labor—tracked, quantified, managed.[^11]

> "When a young Silicon Valley software engineer realizes they're being tracked in the same way a warehouse picker follows instructions, class divisions dissolve."[^11]

Lisa Kresge (丽莎·克雷格), a researcher at UC Berkeley's Labor Center, also pointed out that tech leaders试图制造 "mystification" of AI as a strategy to weaken workers and critics.[^11]

But union data reveals a矛盾的现实: in 2025, only 9.9% of American workers were union members—a near 40-year low.[^12] Anxiety is spreading while collective action is shrinking.

### Generation Z: Doomsday Preppers

In April 2026, The Guardian reported on the phenomenon of Generation Z becoming "preppers"—preparing not for nuclear war but for AI-caused unemployment.[^13]

A 2025 LinkedIn survey showed 63% of executives reported AI would at least partially replace entry-level employees.[^13] Entry-level work—data entry, junior programming, content writing—is the first rung on the ladder for college graduates entering the workforce. If this rung is removed, the career ladder断裂.

The New York Times reported in May 2025: college graduate unemployment rates were rising as companies试图用 AI to replace entry-level workers.[^14] This is not prediction—it's happening now. Generation Z's response: learn multiple skills, maintain flexibility for随时转行. But when everyone is "preparing" and no one is putting down roots, labor relations themselves become fragile.

### Politicization

Fear is being politicized. Democratic strategist David Shor's (大卫·肖尔) data shows: AI job losses will become a 2028 presidential campaign issue.[^15] He tested a political ad: "We'll make companies and billionaires profiting from AI pay their fair share. They work for robots. We work for you."[^15]

Protectionist proposals are emerging: data center bans, autonomous vehicle bans, chatbot bans.[^1] In April 2026, an attacker tried to firebomb Sam Altman's home; another was charged with assaulting a city councilor who approved a data center project.[^1] When institutional responses are absent, violence may fill the vacuum.

## III. Testimonies

### Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO)

Amodei predicted 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs would disappear by 2030, and AI could create an "underclass."[^10] In March 2026, Anthropic established a research institute to study economic impacts, but co-founder Jack Clark (杰克·克拉克) admitted: "The default path is to let technology develop without considering social impacts."[^1] Clark提议 expanding the human份额 in "relational roles" (teaching, nursing)—critics指出 this等于承认 AI will eliminate most cognitive labor, leaving humans with only emotional labor. Anthropic has not released specific economic policies.[^1]

### Sam Altman (OpenAI CEO)

OpenAI released a white paper in April 2026 titled "Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age," proposing a 32-hour work week, increased corporate taxes, a "public wealth fund" (giving citizens AI company equity), and providing foundational models to schools.[^16]

But three problems: implementation mechanisms are极其模糊; OpenAI hasn't committed to advocating these policies; OpenAI removed its profit cap in 2025, while a super PAC funded by president Greg Brockman (格雷格·布洛克曼) spent $2 million opposing AI safety regulation.[^16] OpenAI calls for protecting workers on paper, opposes regulation in action, and pursues unlimited profit in structure.

### Silicon Valley Anonymous Practitioner

> "Most people I know in the AI industry think ordinary people are screwed, and they have absolutely no idea what to do about it."[^1]

Inside the AI industry, there is deep pessimism about the future of human labor, but this pessimism hasn't转化为积极行动 for worker protection. Knowing the ship is sinking but not repairing it—this is Silicon Valley's core paradox.

## IV. Impact and Aftermath

**Economic Consequences of Fear:** When 70% of the population believes AI will reduce job opportunities, this belief itself affects consumption, education choices, and career planning. AI investment accounting for 39% of economic growth means growth increasingly depends on an industry viewed as a threat by seven-tenths of the population.[^5] If the AI bubble bursts, the支柱支撑 growth will collapse.[^17]

**Gap Between Company Actions and Narratives:** Anthropic established a research institute but has no policy立场; OpenAI released a white paper but opposes regulation; Block attributed cutting half its workforce to AI, but a weak crypto market and declining stock price may be the real causes.[^2] AI's role in this case is: a decent excuse.

**The Dilemma of Worker Organization:** Union membership rate at 9.9%, a near 40-year low.[^12] Ford Foundation's Sarita Gupta (萨里塔·古普塔) believes AI anxiety "is creating opportunities for the labor movement."[^11] But opportunities haven't become reality. Fear itself is atomized—when everyone fears being replaced, the response is stockpiling skills, not organizing. Collective action requires not fear but anger and hope.

**Policy Vacuum:** 79% of voters worry the government has no plan to protect workers.[^8] Their worry is justified—the government确实 has no plan. Inaction means letting the market determine AI's impact: whoever owns AI wins, whoever is replaced loses.

## Assessment

### Is Fear Justified, or Amplified?

Both.

Fear is justified because structural changes are occurring. AI确实 is replacing entry-level cognitive labor—college graduate unemployment rates are rising, companies are using AI to replace junior employees—these are happening now.[^14] When 63% of executives report AI will replace entry-level jobs,[^13] this isn't fear-mongering; it's a business plan.

But fear is also amplified. Those amplifying it are precisely those制造 AI. Amodei predicted 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs would disappear by 2030,[^10] without citing any economic model or historical analogy. A CEO selling AI products making judgments about AI's destructive power—doesn't mean he's wrong, but motives need examination.

Fear is the best marketing: when a company tells you "AI will replace your employees," it's actually saying "buy my AI or your competitors will." Fear is also a depoliticization tool: when workers fear AI, they won't ask "why aren't wages rising," only "how do I avoid being replaced." Transforming structural problems of distributive injustice into technical problems is a form of depoliticization.

### How Technological Determinism Exploits Fear

Technological determinism's core creed is: technology develops according to its own logic; society can only adapt, not choose. Altman called AI "unstoppable,"[^1] describing technological development as natural law. But technology has never developed this way—ATMs didn't eliminate bank tellers; bank teller numbers actually increased after ATMs普及. How technology affects employment depends on institutional arrangements, corporate strategies, and social choices.

Technological determinism's danger lies in: it disguises choice as destiny. Kresge's judgment deserves reaffirmation: tech leaders试图制造 "mystification" of AI as a strategy to weaken workers and critics.[^11] When AI is "mystified,"追问 who benefits and who is受损 becomes impossible—because "that's just how technology is."

### From Fear to Action

Fear doesn't automatically generate action. Union membership at 9.9% alongside 64% AI anxiety reveals a断裂: workers are afraid but haven't organized.

Workers need three things: **information**—AI companies are systematically制造信息 asymmetry, and the right to know is the prerequisite for all other rights; **organization**—unions need to更新方式, not just fighting for wages but also for algorithmic transparency rights and AI audit rights; **alternative narratives**—technology's direction is the result of choices, and workers can participate in this choice.

> "We must constantly remind ourselves that the direction of technology is a choice. We can use AI to build a surveillance economy that squeezes the last drop of value from workers, or we can use it to build an era of shared prosperity."[^11]

Choice requires power. Power requires organization. Organization requires hope—not just fear.

Silicon Valley CEOs discuss "permanent underclass" at dinner parties, then return to their companies to继续加速. Karp says "when the country explodes, none of us make money"—his concern is profit-oriented, but his judgment may be correct: a society where seven-tenths of the population views AI as a threat has fragile stability. OpenAI calls for protecting workers on paper but opposes regulation in action.[^16] When proposers' actions contradict their proposals, where does trust come from?

Fear is real. Fear is justified. But fear is not the answer. The answer is power—who has it, who lacks it, and how the latter can obtain it.
