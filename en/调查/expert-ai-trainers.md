# Expert AI Trainers: When White-Collar Knowledge Becomes Training Data

> **As of 2026-08-28. Evidence status: the market for high-skill AI training is documented by multiple reports and platform materials; its long-term displacement effects remain uncertain.**

The public image of data labeling used to be repetitive image boxes, text classification, and toxic-content review. By 2025–2026, a new layer had grown quickly. AI developers no longer needed only people who could say whether an image contained a cat. They increasingly needed lawyers, consultants, engineers, creators, scientists, and recent graduates to turn professional judgment into tasks that models could learn from.

These workers can be described as **expert trainers**. They are not necessarily AI engineers. They supply domain judgment during post-training, evaluation, reinforcement-learning workflows, and agent development.

## What we know

Bloomberg reported in April 2026 that Mercor was recruiting white-collar professionals to teach AI agents routines drawn from their professional work. The report described Mercor as a roughly $10 billion intermediary in this market.[^1]

Bloomberg had already reported in 2025 on recent graduates taking model-training work. One music and cognitive-science graduate said he was paid $50 an hour to teach models music theory and score analysis.[^2] That example is not an industry average, but it confirms that AI training has expanded from basic annotation into the purchase of specialized knowledge.

The work can include:

- writing difficult prompts;
- grading multi-step answers;
- comparing two outputs against professional standards;
- designing failure cases;
- building agent environments;
- checking tool use and workflow execution;
- translating tacit professional judgment into explicit rubrics.

Outlier publicly says expert contributors may generate prompts, rank AI outputs, improve responses, or complete other tasks intended to improve model performance.[^3]

## India as a source of expert training labor

TechCrunch reported in March 2026 that Deccan AI was using an India-centered contributor network for post-training data, evaluations, and reinforcement-learning environments. The company said it had more than one million registered contributors and typically 5,000–10,000 active contributors in a month. Its founder described rates ranging from roughly $10 to $700 per hour depending on specialized demand.[^4]

Those figures are company claims, not independent wage statistics. They should be read as evidence of how the market is structured, not as a typical pay scale.

## Creatives training systems during a weak labor market

The Guardian reported in August 2026 that some Hollywood writers, directors, and producers had accepted AI-training gigs while traditional production opportunities were scarce. Reported rates varied from the low tens to hundreds of dollars per hour.[^5]

The broader decline in film work has many causes, including industry cycles, strikes, costs, and platform strategy. It would be wrong to attribute all of it to AI. The labor issue is narrower: workers whose traditional opportunities are shrinking may be offered short-term income for converting their expertise into training material for systems that could later reduce some demand for that expertise.

## How expert training differs from basic labeling

| Dimension | Basic labeling | Expert training |
|---|---|---|
| Main input | time, attention, rule-following | domain judgment and experience |
| Typical task | classify, box, review | evaluate, critique, design tasks, build agent environments |
| Pay | often low | highly dispersed, sometimes much higher |
| Main risk | low pay, instability, harmful content | knowledge extraction, contract volatility, uncertain future demand |

The two forms coexist. As models improve, some simple labeling can be automated, while advanced models create demand for experts who can identify subtle errors.

## What we do not know

- There is no reliable public estimate of the average hourly earnings of expert trainers.
- A high advertised rate does not establish typical realized income or continuous work.
- Participation in training does not prove that a worker's original occupation will later be automated.
- The long-term career path of expert training is unclear because much of the work is project-based contracting.
- It remains uncertain how much frontier labs will outsource versus bring in-house.

## Analysis

Industrial management converted physical skills into standardized procedures. Platform work converted rides and deliveries into discrete jobs. AI training is beginning to convert professional judgment itself into reusable evaluation signals.

The key question is therefore not whether experts are literally “training their replacements.” It is what happens to occupations whose wages historically depended on professional knowledge being scarce when pieces of that knowledge can be purchased once and reused across millions of model interactions.
