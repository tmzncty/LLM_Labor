# Scale AI Outsourcing: From Labeling Factory to Expert Data Market

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** Scale AI illustrates how AI data labor has shifted from image annotation toward model evaluation, coding, mathematics and expert knowledge. The work has become more skilled, but platform workers still face volatile task supply, opaque access rules and contractor risk.

## What changed

Scale AI, founded in 2016, first became known for labeled data used in fields such as autonomous driving. In the generative-AI era, more of its business moved toward model evaluation, preference data and specialized expert tasks. In June 2025 Meta invested about $14.3 billion for a 49% stake, valuing Scale at roughly $29 billion. Founder Alexandr Wang joined Meta's AI effort while Jason Droege became interim CEO.[^1][^2]

Reuters reported that OpenAI planned to continue using Scale while stressing that it relied on multiple data vendors. OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar also said that increasingly capable models require real domain expertise from academics and specialists.[^3]

## Platform labor remains precarious

Scale-linked platforms such as Remotasks and Outlier rely heavily on distributed contractors. In March 2024 Rest of World reported that Remotasks abruptly cut off many workers in Kenya, Nigeria and Pakistan. Scale cited enhanced security protocols and administrative failures in notification.[^4]

The lesson is not that every worker is low-paid. It is that the platform controls whether work exists at all. Eligibility rules, project access, quality scores and regional policy can rapidly turn a nominal hourly rate into no income.

## Labor-law questions

Reuters reported in March 2025 that the U.S. Department of Labor was investigating Scale AI for possible Fair Labor Standards Act issues involving pay practices and working conditions. An investigation is not a finding of wrongdoing; Scale said it supported contributors and worked to resolve payment issues quickly.[^5]

## The 2025 reversal

One month after the Meta investment, Scale cut about 200 employees—roughly 14% of staff—and ended work with about 500 contractors. Interim CEO Jason Droege said the company had expanded its generative-AI data capacity too quickly.[^6][^7]

This should not be described as “AI replacing Scale workers.” It is better evidence that the AI-data market itself is unstable: changes in customers, training methods and demand propagate directly to the people producing training data.

## A stratified labor market

AI-data work now ranges from simple labeling and transcription to preference ranking, coding, science and adversarial model evaluation. Higher-skill projects may pay much more than traditional microtasks, but high rates do not guarantee stable task supply.

See also: `Expert AI Trainers` and `Outlier Task Workers`.

## What the evidence does not support

- Company valuation is not worker pay.
- Advertised maximum rates are not typical earnings.
- A Labor Department investigation is not proof of a violation.
- The 2025 layoffs cannot be reduced to automation.
- There is no sound basis for claiming all Global South annotators earn the same $1–3 hourly range.
