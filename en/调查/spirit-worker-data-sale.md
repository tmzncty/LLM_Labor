# Spirit Worker Data Sale: When Workplace Records Become AI Assets After Bankruptcy

> **As of 2026-08-28. Status: REVISED.** Google's $10 million proposed purchase of internal Spirit Airlines business data still requires bankruptcy-court approval. The data has not been transferred. This entry documents an ongoing worker-data dispute, not a completed sale.

In August 2026, a rarely discussed AI-labor question became concrete: **who owns the emails, chats, schedules, spreadsheets, and process records workers create while doing their jobs, and can those records later be sold as AI assets when a company collapses?**

Spirit Airlines' bankruptcy put that question before a court.

## What happened

Reuters reported that Google won a $10 million auction for internal business data from bankrupt Spirit Airlines. The proposed dataset includes employee emails, Microsoft Teams messages, spreadsheets, calendars, and marketing, productivity, and operations data. Google plans to use the material for product development and AI-model training.[^1]

Spirit ceased operations in May 2026 and has been selling remaining assets.[^1]

The data sale had **not** been approved as of August 28. A hearing was postponed to September 9 after the Association of Flight Attendants-CWA (AFA) objected.[^1] AFA explicitly told members that the data had not yet been transferred to Google.[^2]

## Why the union objects

Spirit says the data will be de-identified and will not contain personally identifiable information. But AFA argues that the agreement also preserves **referential integrity** across datasets, maintaining relationships among records. The union worries that individuals or small groups could still be reconstructed from context even after direct identifiers are removed.[^2]

AFA says the proposed records include employment and payroll records, email, SharePoint material, Microsoft Teams information, and other workplace data.[^2]

Its key distinction is important: **de-identification addresses whether a record directly identifies a person; it does not necessarily address whether the content itself is confidential.**

## Google's and Spirit's position

Google told WIRED that the data could help improve products and AI models and that Google would not receive personal information from the dataset.[^3] Spirit similarly says the material will be de-identified and will exclude customer and personally identifiable information.[^1]

The current factual state is therefore:

1. Google won the auction for a proposed data transaction;
2. the dataset includes extensive internal work records;
3. Spirit and Google argue de-identification protects personal information;
4. the union argues confidentiality and re-identification risks remain;
5. the court has not approved the transaction.

## Why this belongs in labor history

Work itself continuously produces data: email, chat, scheduling, workflow records, incident logs, internal documentation, performance information, and organizational knowledge.

Those records were created to run an airline. AI gives them a second potential value: they can become examples of how a real organization communicates, schedules, solves problems, and executes processes.

That creates a new distributional question:

> Workers create organizational knowledge in the course of employment; the company owns the systems that record it. If those records are later sold for AI training, what rights to notice, objection, confidentiality, or compensation should workers have?

There is no single legal answer yet.

## Bankruptcy sharpens the problem

Data is unusual as an asset. It is cheap to copy, can persist for decades, may be reused for purposes completely different from those for which it was collected, and can suddenly become more valuable as AI demand grows.

The Spirit dispute may therefore be an early example of a recurring problem: **when a company disappears, the digital traces of its workforce may remain saleable.**

## What we cannot say

- The sale is not complete.
- Google has not received the dataset.
- We cannot say de-identification will fail, or that it is necessarily sufficient.
- Public materials do not establish that Google intends to train a model to replace Spirit workers.
- Risks raised by the union are not evidence that misuse has already occurred.
