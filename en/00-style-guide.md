# Style Guide v2.0

> Revised: 2026-08-28

## 0. General rules

### 0.1 Prose

Use plain modern prose. Be readable and precise. Do not substitute slogans for evidence.

### 0.2 Factual integrity

- Every material factual claim must be traceable.
- Numbers require a date, geography, denominator or measurement basis, and source.
- Single-source claims should be attributed explicitly.
- Never invent interviewees, anonymous testimony, quotations, statistics, legal status, or company statements.
- Distinguish correlation, company attribution, and independently established causation.
- Preserve the anonymity level used by the original source.

### 0.3 People first

Priority order:

1. workers and working conditions;
2. pay, hours, risk, and bargaining power;
3. displacement events and task redesign;
4. unions, contracts, policy, and organizational response;
5. analysis and commentary.

### 0.4 Scope

From 2026-08-28 onward, do not add China-mainland-centered topics. See `../SCOPE.md`.

### 0.5 Evidence levels

Follow `../docs/CLAIM_STANDARD.md`: Documented / Well-supported / Single-source or Pending / Analysis.

## 1. Investigations

Recommended structure:

```text
# [Topic]
> Core finding + as-of date

## Background
## What we know
## Working conditions / worker accounts
## Aftermath and institutions
## What we do not know
## Analysis
## Sources
```

Every investigation should state what the evidence does **not** establish.

## 2. Impact

Do not assume a profession is disappearing. Separate task automation, hiring changes, employment, separations, income, and company announcements.

## 3. Transitions

Cover adaptation, exit, resistance, AI supervision/review work, and collective bargaining.

## 4. Essays

A clear argument is allowed, but facts and value judgments must remain distinguishable and counterevidence must be addressed.

## 5. Data tables

Every consequential row needs a source or footnote.

## 6. Sources

Store source metadata by default. Do not copy full copyrighted news pages into the repository. Public contracts, government documents, and datasets may be archived when lawful and useful.

## 7. Bilingual parity

A new formal Chinese article should normally receive an English counterpart under `en/` using the same factual source set.
