# Financial Analysts

> Financial analysts were once one of Wall Street's most glamorous careers—graduating from prestigious universities, high starting salaries, and a clear path to fund manager. But since 2023, Bloomberg GPT, Morgan Stanley's AI research assistant, and automated financial modeling tools are devouring junior analysts' core work. Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and other investment banks have begun reducing junior analyst headcount. When research reports can be generated with one click from an AI portal, when valuation models can be built by GPT-4 in 10 minutes, a $600 billion industry is redefining the meaning of "analyst."

## I. Before the Impact: What This Profession Used to Be

Financial analysts are the "eyes" of capital markets. They analyze company financial statements, industry trends, and macroeconomic data to provide依据 for investment decisions.

**Professional Categories**:

- **Sell-Side Analysts**: Working at investment banks and brokerages, writing research reports, providing investment recommendations for institutional investors. Representatives: Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, CICC.
- **Buy-Side Analysts**: Working at fund companies and asset management institutions, providing internal research support for fund managers. Representatives: BlackRock, Bridgewater, E Fund.
- **Credit Analysts**: Assessing the credit risk of bond issuers.
- **Quantitative Analysts (Quants)**: Using mathematical models and programming for investment strategy development.

**Market Size**:

In 2023, the global financial services industry market size was approximately $28 trillion[^1], of which research and analysis services accounted for approximately $220 billion. The global securities research market is approximately $15 billion[^2], but the broader financial services部门 employing analysts employs millions of practitioners.

**Number of Practitioners**:

- United States: Approximately 330,000 financial analysts[^3]
- China: Approximately 500,000 securities practitioners, of which approximately 5,000 are licensed analysts[^4]
- Global: Approximately 1.2 million financial analysts[^5]

**Income Levels**:

- U.S. junior analysts (Analyst, 0-3 years): Annual salary $100,000-$150,000 (base + bonus)[^6]
- Senior analysts (VP/Associate, 3-8 years): Annual salary $200,000-$400,000
- Managing Directors (MD): Annual salary $500,000-$2,000,000
- Chinese brokerage analysts: Annual salary 300,000-800,000 RMB, New Fortune award-winning analysts can reach 2-5 million RMB[^7]

**Job Content**:

Junior analysts' daily work is highly repetitive:
- **Financial Modeling**: Building DCF (Discounted Cash Flow), LBO (Leveraged Buyout), comparable company analysis models
- **Data Collection**: Extracting data from Bloomberg Terminal, Wind, company financial statements
- **Writing Research Reports**: Writing analysis results into 20-50 page research reports
- **Updates and Maintenance**: Updating models and reports for all覆盖 companies every quarter during earnings season
- **Making PPTs**: Creating presentations for client meetings and roadshows

A typical investment bank junior analyst works 70-100 hours per week[^8], most of which is spent on Excel modeling and making PPTs.

**Professional Threshold**:

- Typically requires a degree in finance, economics, accounting, or related fields
- CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst) certification is an industry通行证
- Proficiency in Excel modeling and Bloomberg Terminal
- Strong data processing and analysis capabilities

## II. The Impact: What AI Did

The AI化 of financial analysis is not gradual—2023 was a watershed.

**Bloomberg GPT**:

In March 2023, Bloomberg released BloombergGPT, a 50-billion parameter large language model specifically trained for the financial domain[^9]. It can:
- Automatically interpret financial statement data
- Generate financial summaries
- Analyze market sentiment
- Answer complex financial questions

BloombergGPT outperformed general models by more than 20% on financial natural language processing tasks[^10].

**Morgan Stanley's AI Research Assistant**:

In March 2023, Morgan Stanley announced a partnership with OpenAI to deploy an AI assistant for 16,000 financial advisors[^11]. This system can:
- Instantly search and summarize the company's internal 100,000+ research reports
- Provide personalized investment recommendations for clients
- Automatically generate client communication content

Morgan Stanley's Wealth Management head Andy Saperstein said: "This is a game-changing tool."[^12]

**Automated Financial Modeling**:

Multiple AI tools can automatically generate financial models:
- **Microsoft 365 Copilot**: Automatically generates formulas, charts, and analysis in Excel[^13]
- **Daloopa**: AI automatically extracts data from financial statements and directly fills Excel models[^14]
- **FinChat.io**: Query financial data using natural language, automatically generate analysis charts[^15]
- **Wisesheets**: AI-driven financial analysis plugin that automatically builds valuation models[^16]

A DCF model that takes a junior analyst 8-12 hours to build can be completed by AI tools in 10-30 minutes.

**Automated Research Report Generation**:

In 2024, multiple brokerages began using AI to assist in writing research reports:
- **Kensho** (under S&P Global): Automatically generates financial statement analysis summaries[^17]
- **JPMorgan**: AI tools can automatically generate initial drafts of industry research reports[^18]
- **CICC**: Reportedly testing AI-assisted research report writing systems[^19]

According to industry estimates, AI can reduce research report writing time by 60%-70%[^20].

**Quantitative Trading's Squeeze on Subjective Analysis**:

The growth of quantitative trading strategies has further compressed traditional analysts' space:
- In 2023, assets under management by quantitative funds exceeded $1.5 trillion[^21]
- Quantitative strategies' market share grew from 15% in 2010 to 35% in 2023[^22]
- Multiple traditional fund companies (such as Fidelity, T. Rowe Price) have reduced their subjective analyst teams

## III. The Numbers: Layoffs/Income Decline/Industry Scale Changes

**Investment Bank Layoff Wave**:

2023-2025, layoffs at major global investment banks:

| Company | Layoffs | Date | Main Affected Positions |
|---------|---------|------|------------------------|
| Goldman Sachs | ~3,200 | January 2023 | Junior analysts, middle and back office[^23] |
| Morgan Stanley | ~3,000 | June 2023 | Junior analysts, operations[^24] |
| Citigroup | ~20,000 | 2024 (planned) | Across-the-board cuts[^25] |
| Barclays | ~2,000 | 2024 | Investment banking division[^26] |

**Junior Analyst Position Reductions**:

According to Coalition Greenwich data:[^27]
- 2022: The top 12 global investment banks employed approximately 18,000 junior analysts
- 2025 (projected): Decrease to approximately 14,000, a reduction of 22%

**Salary Changes**:

While U.S. junior investment bank analyst salaries are nominally still rising (partly due to talent competition), bonuses have significantly shrunk:[^28]
- 2021: Junior analyst average bonus approximately $100,000-$150,000 (bull market)
- 2023: Junior analyst average bonus dropped to $50,000-$80,000
- 2024: Some investment banks reduced bonus pools by 20%-30%

**Changes in Brokerage Research Departments**:

Changes in Chinese brokerage research departments are particularly显著:[^29]
- Since 2023, multiple brokerages have reduced research institute headcount
- Analyst "commission sharing" income decreased by approximately 30%
- Some small and medium brokerage research institutes have begun merging or closing

**Industry Landscape Changes**:

According to an Opimas report, by 2025, AI will cause global financial institutions to reduce approximately 230,000 positions[^30], of which research and analysis positions account for approximately 15%.

## V. Response: Who Adapted, Who Left

**Those Who Adapted**:

- **AI-Augmented Analysts**: Proficient in using AI tools, improving efficiency by 3-5x, focusing on high-value judgment work
- **Strategic Analysts**: Shifting from "building models" to "interpreting models," from data processing to investment观点
- **Cross-Domain Talent**: Demand for analysts with finance + AI复合 backgrounds surged, with salary premiums of 30%-50%[^37]
- **Client Relationship-Oriented**: Shifting from back-office analysis to front-office client relationship management—AI cannot replace trust

**Those Who Left**:

- **Junior Modelers**: Replaced by the combination of AI + a small number of senior analysts
- **Data Collectors**: Tools like Bloomberg AI and Daloopa have made manual collection redundant
- **Template Research Report Writers**: Standardized research reports are generated by AI; only differentiated观点 have value
- **Career Transition Directions**: Strategic analysis at tech companies, AI fintech startups, post-investment management at PE/VC

**Areas Still Standing**:

- **Deep Industry Research**: Requires field research, management interviews, supply chain judgment[^38]
- **Complex Transaction Structure Design**: Non-standardized transactions like M&A and restructuring still require human experience[^39]
- **Investor Relations**: Trust relationships with listed company management cannot be replaced by AI
- **Emerging Market Analysis**: Markets with scarce data and information asymmetry still rely on human judgment[^40]

**The Moat Effect of CFA**:

CFA Institute data shows that CFA exam registration numbers were still growing in 2023[^41]—but the average salary growth of certification holders has明显 slowed. The value of CFA as "skill certification" has not decreased, but the market value of "skills" themselves is being redefined by AI.

## Assessment

The story of financial analysts' impact is本质上 a story about **the disappearance of the "middle layer."**

The hierarchical structure of investment banks works like this: Managing Directors (MDs) are responsible for bringing in clients and making decisions; Vice Presidents (VPs) are responsible for project management; Junior analysts are responsible for grunt work—modeling, making PPTs, collecting data. This pyramid has运转 for thirty years, with each layer having clear value.

AI doesn't attack from the top—it devours from the bottom. It eats junior analysts' work: modeling, data collection, writing template reports. But the problem is, this "grunt work" is precisely the only path for junior analysts to become senior analysts. A VP must have经历过 the stage of building models to understand a model's limitations; an MD must have经历过 the process of writing research reports to judge a report's credibility.

**When AI replaces basic training, it doesn't just replace a job—it cuts off the ladder of talent growth.**

This is different from the accounting industry's story. Accounting has a license system for protection (signing authority), translation has cultural barriers for protection (localization). But financial analysts—especially junior analysts—have几乎 no institutional protection. No法定 signing authority, no license requirements; CFA is just a "bonus" rather than an "admission ticket." This means the market can freely replace junior analysts with AI without any legal barriers.

But the impact on financial analysis also reveals a deeper paradox: **when everyone uses AI for analysis, the value of analysis反而 disappears.**

In the era of information scarcity, an analyst's value lay in being able to获取 and process information faster and more accurately than others. But when AI makes information processing free and instantaneous, this ability is no longer a competitive advantage—it becomes basic infrastructure. The true competitive advantage becomes what AI cannot do: judgment about company management, intuition about industry拐点, grasp of market sentiment.

The problem is, the market prices these "advanced capabilities" far lower than "basic grunt work"—because the latter is quantifiable and the former is not. An analyst who can build a perfect DCF model is更容易 recognized by the market than an analyst with good intuition but no modeling skills. AI is overturning this pricing logic—it forces the industry to admit: modeling is not value, judgment is. But the industry is not yet ready to pay for "judgment."

The story of financial analysts is a预演 for all "knowledge workers": **your skills won't disappear—they'll be reduced to the price of tools.**
