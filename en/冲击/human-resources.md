# Human Resources: AI Does Not Erase HR, but Hiring Algorithms Become a Labor-Rights Issue

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** Resume-screening automation should not be translated into “HR will disappear.” Current U.S. projections show human-resources specialists growing 6%. The deeper change is that AI now participates directly in recruiting, screening and worker management, shifting some HR labor from processing applications to governing systems that affect people's careers.

BLS projects human-resources specialists to grow 6% from 2025 to 2035, with about 73,700 openings per year.[^1] Lower-level HR assistants show a different pattern: BLS projects human-resources assistants (excluding payroll/timekeeping) to decline about 6%.[^2]

SHRM's 2025 Talent Trends survey of 2,040 U.S. HR professionals found 43% of organizations using AI for HR tasks, up from 26% in 2024. Recruiting was the most common area, with 51% using AI support.[^3]

Among those organizations, common uses included job-description writing (66%), resume screening (44%), candidate search (32%), job-post customization (31%) and applicant communication (29%). Eighty-nine percent of HR respondents using AI in recruiting said it saved time or increased efficiency.[^4]

Those are adoption data, not evidence of a specific HR headcount reduction.

Employment AI also carries unusual governance consequences because it affects access to jobs. The EU AI Act classifies certain systems used for recruitment, selection, promotion, termination, task allocation and worker monitoring as high-risk because they can affect livelihoods, privacy and discrimination.[^5][^6]

AI therefore creates new HR work around validation, explanation, appeals, discrimination testing and deciding which employment decisions should remain human.

## Evidence boundaries

- SHRM figures describe a U.S. HR-professional survey.
- AI adoption rates are not layoff rates.
- A decline in HR assistants is not a decline in HR specialists.
- EU high-risk classification does not mean every employment AI system is unlawful or discriminatory.
- Resume ranking does not replace employee relations, labor negotiation or organizational judgment.
