# Journalists

> The news industry was already severely wounded in the internet age—advertising revenue was eroded by Google and Facebook, and print media closed en masse. AI is the second wave of impact. When AI can generate a standard news release in seconds, when Buzzfeed uses AI to write articles and lays off 12% of staff, when the Associated Press automates corporate earnings reports with AI—journalists face not "efficiency improvement" but an "existential crisis."

## I. Before the Impact: What This Profession Used to Be

Journalists are the executors of the "Fourth Estate"—overseeing government, exposing corruption, recording history, and serving the public interest.

**The Golden Age of Journalism (1950-2000)**:
- U.S. newspaper advertising revenue grew from $2 billion in 1950 to $48 billion in 2000[^1]
- Newsrooms were fully staffed, and investigative journalists had months to work on a single story
- Journalism was a respected profession with upper-middle-class income

**The Internet's First Impact (2000-2020)**:
- Craigslist destroyed classified advertising revenue
- Google and Facebook swallowed the digital advertising market
- U.S. newsrooms shrank from approximately 75,000 in 2000 to approximately 30,000 in 2020[^2]
- Between 2000-2020, over 2,000 U.S. newspapers closed[^3]

**What Journalists Do**:
- News reporting: Writing news articles, interviewing subjects
- Investigative reporting: In-depth research, data analysis, document review
- Commentary and analysis: Writing editorials, columns, analytical articles
- Feature writing: Long-form narrative, personality profiles, on-scene reporting

**Income Levels**:
- U.S. journalist median annual salary: approximately $55,000[^4]
- Chinese journalist average monthly salary: approximately 8,000-15,000 RMB[^5]
- Freelance journalist income is unstable, often below local average wages

## II. The Impact: What AI Did

After 2023, generative AI's impact on journalism expanded from "automating simple reports" to "threatening entire newsrooms."

**Automated News Writing**:

The Associated Press has used AI to automatically generate corporate earnings reports since 2014.[^6] What previously took journalists 30 minutes to write—earnings summaries—AI completes in seconds. By 2023, the AP generates over 40,000 corporate earnings reports annually using AI.

**BuzzFeed's AI Experiment**:

In January 2023, BuzzFeed announced it would use AI to generate content and laid off 12% of its staff (approximately 180 people).[^7] BuzzFeed CEO Jonah Peretti stated: "AI will enhance content creation, not replace creators." But the reality was: among the 180 laid off, many were content creators.

BuzzFeed subsequently launched AI-generated travel guides and personalized content. The quality of this content was widely criticized as "mediocre" and "lacking soul."[^8]

**News Organization AI Deployments**:

| Organization | AI Application | Impact |
|-------------|----------------|--------|
| Associated Press | Corporate earnings automation | Reduced demand for earnings reporters |
| Washington Post | Heliograf (automated news writing system) | Sports events, election results automation |
| Reuters | Lynx Insight (AI-assisted journalism tool) | Journalist efficiency improved, but reduced entry-level positions |
| CNET | AI-generated personal finance articles | Criticized for factual errors, later暂停 |
| Sports Illustrated | AI-generated articles (using fake author names) | Editor-in-chief resigned after scandal |

**CNET Scandal**:

In January 2023, CNET was discovered to have used AI to generate dozens of personal finance articles under fictitious author names.[^9] Investigation revealed these AI articles contained multiple factual errors and instances of plagiarism. CNET暂停 AI writing, but the damage was done.

**Sports Illustrated Scandal**:

In November 2023, Sports Illustrated was discovered to have used AI to generate articles and created fake author profiles for AI (including fake photos and fake resumes).[^10] After the scandal broke, the editor-in-chief resigned, and the magazine's reputation was severely damaged.

## V. Response: Who Adapted, Who Left

**AI-Assisted Journalism**:

Some journalists successfully transitioned to "AI-assisted journalists," using AI as a tool:
- Using AI to quickly generate initial drafts, then manually revising
- Using AI for data analysis and trend identification
- Using AI to translate and search international sources
- Using AI to generate personalized content

**Fields Still Requiring Humans**:

- **Investigative Reporting**: Requires networking, document review, in-depth research[^17]
- **On-Scene Reporting**: Requires being present, observing details, capturing emotions[^18]
- **Commentary and Analysis**: Requires unique perspective, deep thinking, personal style[^19]
- **Interviewing**: Requires building trust, guiding conversation, understanding subtext[^20]

**Journalists Who Left the Industry**:

Many journalists chose to change careers:
- Transitioned to public relations and corporate communications ("reverse crossing"—from journalism to PR)
- Transitioned to content marketing (creating content for brands)
- Transitioned to social media management
- Completely left the media industry

## Assessment

The news industry is the most ironic case in the AI冲击.

The core function of journalism is **revealing truth**. But in the face of AI冲击, the news industry doesn't even dare reveal its own truth. BuzzFeed said "AI will enhance content creation," then laid off 180 content creators. Sports Illustrated said "AI is just a tool," then published AI articles under fake names. News organizations use AI to replace journalists while telling the public "AI won't replace journalists."

This is not a technical problem—it's a power problem. AI冲击 journalism is本质上 another instance of capital compressing labor. The news industry's business model was already destroyed by the internet—advertising revenue was eroded by Google and Facebook, and subscription revenue growth was slow. AI gave news organizations a "solution": use machines to replace people, reduce costs, and maintain profits.

But this "solution" destroyed the reason for journalism's existence.

When AI-generated news releases replace journalist reporting, who will expose government corruption? When AI-generated commentary replaces columnist analysis, who will challenge the abuse of power? When AI-generated features replace long-form narrative, who will record ordinary people's stories?

AI can generate "correct" news—accurate data, correct facts, standard format. But it cannot generate "important" news—investigative reports requiring months of digging, on-scene reports requiring physical presence, commentary requiring unique perspective.

The deeper question is: what is the value of journalism?

If the value of news is just "information"—scores, data, facts—then AI确实 can replace journalists. But if the value of news is "judgment"—what's important, what's not, what's truth, what's lies—then AI cannot replace journalists.

The AP automated corporate earnings reports with AI. This "freed" journalists to do "more valuable" work. But where is this "more valuable" work? When newsrooms shrank from 75,000 to 25,000, where did those "freed" journalists go?

The answer is: many went to the PR industry. A journalist's typical transition path is: from the newsroom to the corporate PR department. Higher income, less pressure, but the nature of work completely changed—from "revealing truth" to "packaging truth."

This is not the failure of journalists. It's the failure of the system. If a society cannot provide a sustainable business model for labor that oversees power, then the "Fourth Estate" becomes an empty口号.

AI accelerated this process. It's not the root cause—the root cause is that journalism's business model already collapsed in the internet age. But AI made the collapse faster, deeper, and more irreversible. (See "Table: AI Layoff Timeline")
