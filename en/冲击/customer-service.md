# Customer Service: AI Takes Routine Contacts First, but “700 Workers' Work” Is Not “700 People Fired”

> **As of 2026-08-28. v2 REVISED.** The previous article incorrectly turned Klarna's “work equivalent to 700 full-time agents” into a claim that 700 specific customer-service employees had been fired and replaced. Fabricated worker stories have also been removed.

## Klarna's 700 figure

Klarna said in February 2024 that its AI assistant handled about 2.3 million conversations in its first month, roughly two-thirds of customer-service chats, and performed work the company estimated was equivalent to 700 full-time agents. It also reported customer satisfaction on par with human agents, fewer repeat inquiries, and shorter resolution times.[^1]

Those are **company-reported operating metrics**. They do not establish that 700 people were fired that month.

Klarna's total workforce did later shrink substantially. In 2025 CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski said the company had moved from roughly 5,500 employees to about 3,000 over two years through attrition and reduced hiring, while also arguing that customers should retain access to human service.[^2] That company-wide change cannot all be assigned to customer-service automation.

## A hybrid structure is emerging

Automation increasingly routes routine contacts to AI while humans handle:

- complex refunds and disputes;
- emotionally sensitive cases;
- inconsistent information across systems;
- corrections after an AI failure;
- high-value customers and exceptions;
- evaluation and improvement of automated support.

When easy cases disappear, the remaining human queue can become harder rather than easier.

## Occupational data shows gradual automation pressure

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects Customer Service Representative employment to decline about 5% from 2024 to 2034, from roughly 2.81 million to 2.66 million. BLS links the decline to automation, self-service, and mobile applications rather than generative AI alone. At the same time, it projects about 341,700 openings per year because workers leave or change occupations.[^3]

A declining occupation can therefore still generate large numbers of real vacancies.

## More explicit company attribution

In September 2025 Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff said the company had reduced about 4,000 customer-support jobs and that AI agents were handling 30%–50% of the relevant workload.[^4]

That is a direct company attribution, not an industry-wide causal estimate.

## What we do not know

- Klarna's 700 figure is a workload equivalent, not an audited net-layoff figure.
- Klarna and Salesforce do not represent every support organization.
- Customer-service employment is also shaped by self-service, outsourcing, industry demand, and economic cycles.
- Company quality and satisfaction metrics require independent validation.
- The total cost of automated service includes model operation, escalation, errors, and human review.

## Analysis

Customer service makes the measurement problem unusually visible. AI can handle a countable volume of contacts, but a volume-equivalent does not identify which human jobs disappeared.

A useful archive therefore separates workload, headcount, hiring, attrition, and explicit layoffs rather than collapsing them into one “replacement” number.
