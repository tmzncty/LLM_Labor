# Teachers: AI Is Becoming a Second Instructional Infrastructure, Not an Electronic Teacher

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** Products such as AI tutors do not establish that teachers are being replaced. Teaching combines explanation with diagnosis, motivation, classroom management, assessment responsibility, safeguarding and social relationships. Some instructional tasks automate more readily than the profession as a whole.

BLS explicitly identifies AI/LLM digital tutors as a technology likely to limit growth in tutoring. U.S. tutor employment is projected to grow only about 1% from 2024 to 2034.[^1][^2] That is evidence about tutoring, not all teachers.

Other teaching labor remains in strong demand. BLS projects postsecondary-teacher employment to grow 7% from 2025 to 2035, with about 103,300 openings per year.[^3]

Globally, UNESCO continued in 2025 to emphasize a teacher shortage of roughly 44 million primary and secondary teachers needed by 2030. Its policy position is that AI should augment teachers rather than remove them from education.[^4][^5]

The more credible labor transformation is task redistribution: AI-generated lesson drafts and exercises, chatbot-first student questions, new verification burdens, redesign of assessment in response to AI-written work, and responsibility for teaching students how to evaluate machine output.

## Evidence boundaries

- Slow tutor growth is not evidence about all teachers.
- UNESCO's teacher shortage is a demand estimate, not an AI causal study.
- Strong performance by an AI tutor on a benchmark does not reproduce classroom labor.
- Postsecondary growth does not describe every K–12 system.
- Teacher layoffs caused by demographics or budgets should not be relabeled as AI layoffs.
