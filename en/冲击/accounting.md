# Accounting: Automation Compresses Clerical Work, Not the Entire Profession

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** The old article treated Big Four AI deployment as evidence that accountants were disappearing. Current U.S. projections show a more stratified picture: accountants and auditors still grow, while bookkeeping/accounting clerks decline as routine software automation expands.

BLS projects accountants and auditors to grow 5% from 2025 to 2035, from about 1.60 million to 1.67 million jobs. BLS explicitly says AI and other technologies will automate routine tasks but are not expected to reduce overall demand, instead shifting work toward analysis and advisory responsibilities.[^1]

By contrast, bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks are projected to decline 6% over the same period, or about 85,600 jobs, with technological automation a central reason.[^2]

The Big Four are also changing their skill mix. A Financial Times analysis of more than 50,000 job advertisements found that in 2025 the firms posted more ads for AI specialists than auditors across English-speaking markets; nearly 7% of listings required AI skills, up from under 2% in 2022.[^3] That does not mean audit hiring stopped: the same analysis notes audit teams continue to grow.

UK audit regulator FRC issued AI-in-audit guidance in 2025 and expanded it to generative and agentic AI in 2026. Its 2025 audit-quality review said GenAI remained largely in support functions rather than representing the transformative shift some expected.[^4][^5][^6]

## Evidence boundaries

- A 6% decline for clerks is not a 6% decline for professional accountants.
- 5% projected growth does not mean accountants' work is unchanged.
- Job advertisements are not identical to hires.
- Regulatory guidance does not prove AI improves audit quality.
- Firm layoffs should not be labeled AI-caused without evidence.
