# Legal Assistants: Research and Drafting Automate Faster Than Responsibility

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** Legal AI is now mainstream professional technology, but “Harvey will replace paralegals” is not an established fact. The latest U.S. BLS projection is more restrained: paralegal/legal-assistant employment is essentially flat from 2025 to 2035, while AI-driven productivity is explicitly expected to limit demand.

BLS reports about 404,900 paralegals and legal assistants in 2025 and projects roughly 403,800 in 2035, rounded to 0% change. About 40,800 openings per year are still expected, largely replacing workers who leave the occupation.[^1]

BLS specifically identifies AI tools for contract review, discovery and legal research as productivity technologies that may constrain employment growth.[^1][^2]

Thomson Reuters' 2025 professional-services survey found GenAI use among legal professionals rising from 14% in 2024 to 26% in 2025, with research, summarization, review and drafting among common uses.[^3] By August 2026 Google was expanding Gemini Enterprise for Legal and integrating with systems including Thomson Reuters, Harvey and Legora.[^4]

Yet responsibility remains human. ABA Formal Opinion 512 says lawyers using GenAI retain duties of competence, confidentiality, communication, reasonable fees and supervision, including supervision of subordinate lawyers and paralegals using AI.[^5]

There is also strong counterevidence to a simple collapse story: a Wells Fargo survey reported by Reuters found large U.S. law-firm revenues up 12.4% in the first half of 2026, billable-hour demand up 4.8% and lawyer headcount up 2.9%.[^6]

## Evidence boundaries

- BLS 0% is a U.S. decade projection, not a universal no-impact claim.
- Vendor adoption surveys do not establish layoffs caused by AI.
- Law-firm revenue growth does not guarantee better outcomes for support staff.
- Ethical obligations require oversight but do not prove every firm governs AI well.

The labor question is increasingly about training: if machines absorb research and first-draft work, how do junior legal workers accumulate the detailed experience needed to supervise them later?
