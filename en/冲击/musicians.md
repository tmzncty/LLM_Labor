# Musicians

> Music is one of humanity's oldest art forms. From Beethoven to the Beatles, from jazz to hip-hop, music has always been an expression of human emotion and creativity. But AI is changing all of this. When Suno can generate a complete song in seconds, when AI can mimic any singer's voice, when record companies begin using AI to generate "virtual artists"—musicians face not a "new tool" but an "existential crisis."

## I. Before the Impact: What This Profession Used to Be

Musicians are a diverse group—from classical musicians to pop singers, from independent musicians to film score composers.

**Music Industry Size**:
- In 2023, global music industry revenue was approximately $28 billion[^1]
- Of which streaming revenue was approximately $17 billion (60%)
- Live performance revenue was approximately $30 billion (independent statistics)[^2]

**Musician Income Distribution**:

Musician income is highly unequal:
- Top tier (Top 1%): Annual income of millions to hundreds of millions of dollars (Taylor Swift, BTS, etc.)
- Mid-tier (professional musicians): Annual income of $30,000-$100,000
- Bottom tier (independent musicians): Annual income below $10,000[^3]

According to the American Federation of Musicians (AFM), the median annual income for professional musicians in the U.S. is approximately $40,000-$50,000.[^4] Over 50% of musicians need part-time work to make a living.

**What Musicians Do**:
- Composing and arranging
- Recording and production
- Live performances
- Copyright management and licensing
- Teaching (the primary income source for many musicians)

## II. The Impact: What AI Did

After 2023, AI music generation technology made breakthrough progress.

**Suno and Udio**:

Suno is an AI music generation tool launched in late 2023.[^5] Users只需 enter a text description (such as "a pop song about heartbreak"), and Suno can generate a complete song in seconds—including melody, harmony, lyrics, arrangement, and vocals.

Udio is another similar AI music tool launched in early 2024.[^6]

**Technical Capabilities**:
- Generate music in any style (pop, rock, classical, electronic, hip-hop, etc.)
- Simulate different vocal styles
- Generate lyrics
- Arrange and mix

**AI Music Quality**:

In 2024, a completely AI-generated song "Heart on My Sleeve" received millions of plays on Spotify.[^7] This song mimicked the voices of Drake and The Weeknd, with such high quality that many people couldn't distinguish it from the real thing.

**AI's Impact on Music Creation**:

| Field | AI Capability | Human Advantage | Impact Level |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Background Music | High | Low | High |
| Advertising Music | High | Low | High |
| Game Soundtracks | Medium-High | Medium | Medium |
| Pop Music | Medium | Medium-High | Medium |
| Classical Music | Low-Medium | High | Low |
| Live Performance | None | High | Low |

## III. The Numbers: Layoffs/Income Decline/Industry Scale Changes

**AI Music Flooding Streaming Platforms**:

According to Spotify data:[^8]
- 2023: Approximately 100,000 new tracks uploaded daily
- 2024: Increased to approximately 120,000 daily
- The proportion of AI-generated tracks is undisclosed, but industry estimates range from 5-15%

**Record Company Responses**:

Universal Music Group (UMG) in 2023 asked streaming platforms to block AI from using its copyrighted content to train models.[^9] But the effect was limited—AI models had already been trained on大量 copyrighted music.

**Impact on Independent Musicians**:

According to a survey by the French Musicians' Union:[^10]
- 2023: Independent musicians' average income decreased by 10%
- 2024: Further decreased by 15%
- Main reason: Reduced orders for advertising and background music

## V. Response: Who Adapted, Who Left

**AI as a Creative Tool**:

Some musicians have begun using AI as a creative tool:
- Using AI to generate inspiration and initial drafts
- Using AI for arranging and mixing
- Using AI to explore new musical styles
- "Collaborating" with AI to create

**Fields Still Requiring Humans**:

- **Live Performances**: Audiences want to see human performers, not AI[^14]
- **Musical Narrative**: The stories and emotional connections behind songs[^15]
- **Cultural Innovation**: Creating new musical styles and genres[^16]
- **Music Education**: Human-to-human guidance and inspiration[^17]

**Copyright Lawsuits**:

In 2024, Universal Music Group and other music rights holders sued Suno and Udio, accusing them of using copyrighted music without authorization to train AI models.[^18] As of late 2025, the case is still under trial.

## Assessment

The AI impact on the music industry has a unique dimension: **it threatens not only musicians' livelihoods but also the meaning of music itself.**

The impact on the translation industry is "functional replacement"—AI can translate words but cannot translate culture. The impact on the illustration industry is "style replacement"—AI can mimic styles but cannot create them. But the impact on the music industry is "existential replacement"—when AI can generate music as good as humans, the identity of "musician" itself is questioned.

A musician's value is not just "creating music"—but "expressing human emotions through music." When you listen to Taylor Swift's songs, you're not just hearing melody and lyrics—you're hearing her story, her emotions, her life. AI can mimic Taylor Swift's voice and style, but it cannot replicate Taylor Swift's life.

But the question is: does the market care?

Most plays on Spotify come from "background music"—music for work, exercise, sleeping. For these scenarios, AI-generated music and human-created music are indistinguishable. When the market doesn't care "who created the music," the musician's "unique value" disappears.

Suno's emergence accelerated this trend. Previously, generating a song required a composer, arranger, recording engineer, and mixing engineer. Now, a user inputs one sentence and gets a complete song in seconds. This process requires no musical skills—not even the ability to sing.

This means: the threshold for music creation has dropped from "requiring years of training" to "just knowing how to type." When the threshold drops this low, the "professional value" of musicians is diluted.

Copyright lawsuits are musicians' last line of defense (see "Transformation: Who Is Resisting"). If courts rule that AI music training infringes copyright, AI companies may need to pay for training data. But this defense line may come too late—AI models have already been trained on大量 copyrighted music, and even if courts make a判决, they cannot "undo" already learned knowledge.

Live performances may be musicians' last refuge. Audiences want to see human performers—the sweat on Taylor Swift's stage, the interaction between band members, the unpredictability of live shows. AI cannot replace these. But live performance revenue accounts for only a small portion of the music industry—most musicians live on recording and copyright income.

The future of the music industry may look like this: top-tier musicians (Taylor Swift, BTS) continue to thrive because they sell not just music but identity and story; bottom-tier musicians are淘汰 by AI because their work can be replaced by AI; mid-tier musicians are compressed, going from "creators" to "AI reviewers" or "live performers."

This is not the progress of music. This is the industrialization of music.
