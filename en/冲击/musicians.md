# Musicians: AI Music Floods Supply, but Labor Effects Remain Uneven

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** AI music creates real competition and rights disputes, but “songs can be generated in seconds” does not establish the disappearance of musicians. Current evidence shows pressure on some creative workers while aggregate global music royalties continue to grow.

Reuters reported in January 2026 that Deezer was receiving roughly 60,000 fully AI-generated tracks per day, about 39% of new uploads. The platform said it had flagged more than 13.4 million AI-generated tracks during 2025 and removed large volumes of suspicious AI streams from royalty calculations.[^1]

That demonstrates industrial-scale supply, not equivalent listening share or human-job replacement.

Research cited by the Musicians' Union in 2025 found that among surveyed creative workers, 55% reported diminished financial compensation because of GenAI, 68% reported reduced job security and 61% felt the value of their work had been diminished.[^2] The sample covered creative workers broadly and should not be relabeled as a musician-only prevalence estimate.

Important counterevidence comes from CISAC's 2025 Global Collections Report: creator royalties reached a record €13.97 billion in 2024, up 6.6%, while music collections rose 7.2% to €12.59 billion.[^3] AI disruption and aggregate market growth can therefore coexist.

A CISAC/PMP Strategy model projects that under its assumptions, 24% of music-creator revenue could be at risk by 2028.[^4] That is a forecast, not an observed loss.

The labor effects are likely to concentrate in library music, advertising, demos, low-cost production, voice/style licensing and platform discovery rather than uniformly across all musical work.

## Evidence boundaries

- AI upload share is not listening or revenue share.
- Creative-worker surveys are not universal musician statistics.
- CISAC's 24% figure is a model forecast.
- Record aggregate royalties do not mean every creator is prospering.
- Copyright litigation is not itself an employment metric.
