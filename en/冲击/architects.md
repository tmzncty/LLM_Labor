# Architects

> Architectural design used to be "art wearing a hard hat"—requiring seven years of professional education, years of internship, and passing registration exams before one could practice independently. But since 2023, AI generative design tools (Autodesk Forma, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion) are eroding the core of architectural design: scheme generation. A conceptual scheme that takes a junior architect a week to complete can be generated by AI in dozens of variants within minutes. Large design institutes have begun reducing scheme team headcounts, and "use AI to generate drawings first, then have architects modify" is becoming the new workflow. When design changes from "creation" to "selection," the five-thousand-year-old profession of architecture is experiencing an unprecedented identity crisis.

## I. Before the Impact: What This Profession Used to Be

Architecture is one of humanity's oldest professions. From the pyramids of ancient Egypt to modern skyscrapers, architecture is both technology and art. In many cultures, architects enjoy extremely high social status—they shape the face of cities and define people's living spaces.

**Professional Categories**:

- **Scheme Architects**: Responsible for conceptual design and spatial planning of buildings—this is the "creative core"
- **Construction Drawing Architects**: Transform schemes into constructable drawings, handling technical details
- **Project Architects**: Manage the entire design and construction process
- **Urban Planners**: Participate in larger-scale spatial planning
- **Interior Architects**: Focus on interior space design

**Market Size**:

- In 2023, the global architectural design services market was valued at approximately $380 billion[^1]
- The U.S. architectural design market is approximately $75 billion[^2]
- China's architectural design market is approximately 800 billion RMB[^3]

**Number of Practitioners**:

- United States: Approximately 120,000 licensed architects, approximately 170,000 architectural practitioners[^4]
- UK: Approximately 42,000 registered architects[^5]
- China: Approximately 550,000 first-class registered architects, approximately 2 million architectural design practitioners[^6]
- Global: Approximately 3.3 million architectural practitioners[^7]

**Income Levels**:

- United States: Junior architects (Intern Architect) approximately $50,000-$60,000 annually, licensed architects approximately $80,000-$120,000, partners up to $150,000-$300,000[^8]
- UK: Junior architects approximately £25,000-£35,000 annually, senior architects £50,000-£80,000[^9]
- China: Junior architects approximately 6,000-10,000 RMB monthly, first-class registered architects 15,000-30,000 RMB[^10]

**Professional Threshold**:

Architecture is one of the few design professions requiring strict professional certification:
- United States: Architecture degree (B.Arch 5 years or M.Arch 2-3 years) + internship (AXP 3,740 hours) + exams (ARE 6 sections)[^11]
- UK: RIBA Part 1/2/3 three stages, totaling over 7 years[^12]
- China: Architecture undergraduate 5 years + internship + first-class registered architect exam (9 subjects)[^13]

**Workflow**:

The traditional architectural design process is highly dependent on human labor:
1. **Conceptual Design** (2-4 weeks): Hand-drawn sketches, massing models, spatial exploration
2. **Scheme Development** (4-8 weeks): CAD modeling, rendering, code compliance checking
3. **Preliminary Design** (4-8 weeks): Structural, MEP, HVAC coordination
4. **Construction Drawing Design** (8-16 weeks): Detailed construction drawings
5. **Construction Coordination** (throughout construction period): On-site coordination, design changes

A medium-scale project (such as an office building) typically takes 6-12 months from scheme to construction drawings, involving 10-30 architects.

## II. The Impact: What AI Did

**Generative Design Schemes**:

AI's greatest impact on architectural design lies in "scheme generation"—this was considered architects' most core creative work.

- **Autodesk Forma** (formerly Spacemaker): AI urban planning and architectural design tool that can automatically generate building layout schemes meeting constraints such as lighting, wind environment, and noise[^14]. In 2023, Autodesk integrated it into the Forma platform.
- **Autodesk Generative Design**: Input design constraints (area, floor height, structural requirements, etc.), and AI automatically generates hundreds of schemes meeting conditions[^15].
- **Midjourney / Stable Diffusion**: Architects have begun using AI image generation tools to quickly produce conceptual renderings[^16]. Overnight, 100+ scheme variants can be generated, which previously required a team several weeks of work.
- **Architechtures**: An AI platform specifically for architectural design that can generate complete scheme designs for residential projects in minutes[^17].

**Evolution of Parametric Design**:

Parametric design has been developing for over a decade (representative tool: Grasshopper for Rhino), but AI has推进 it to a new stage:
- Previously: Architects set parameters and rules, software generated schemes—requiring deep programming and design skills
- Now: AI can automatically learn design patterns and automatically generate and optimize schemes—no programming ability required[^18]

**AI-Assisted Construction Drawings**:

- **TestFit**: AI automatically performs building site planning and feasibility analysis, completing in minutes what previously took a week[^19]
- **Swapp**: AI automates construction drawing design, claiming to reduce construction drawing production time by 70%[^20]
- **Hypar**: AI-assisted building component design and coordination platform[^21]

**Chinese Architectural Design AI Tools**:

- **Xiaoku Technology**: AI urban planning and architectural scheme design platform[^22]
- **PiLan**: AI architectural drawing review tool[^23]
- **Terminus Group**: Smart building AI platform[^24]

**The Core of Impact—Revaluation of Scheme Design Value**:

Scheme design (conceptual design) used to be the most "value-added"环节 for architects—it required creativity, spatial intuition, and aesthetic素养. But now:
- AI can generate dozens of schemes meeting functional constraints in minutes
- Architects' work has changed from "creating schemes" to "selecting and modifying from AI schemes"
- Scheme design工时 has significantly缩短, and报价随之 declined

## III. The Numbers: Layoffs/Income Decline/Industry Scale Changes

**Industry Employment Changes**:

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics:[^25]
- 2022: The U.S. had 127,900 architects
- 2032 (projected): Grow to 133,200, growth of only 4.2%
- Comparison: The 2012-2022 growth rate was 8.1%
- While growth slows, AI tools are changing how every architect works

**Design Institute Layoffs and Downsizing**:

Changes in China's architectural design industry are particularly剧烈:
- Since 2023, under the双重 impact of real estate downturn and AI tools, large design institutes have普遍 downsized[^26]
- A leading design institute reportedly reduced employees from 3,000 to 2,000 (unverified)
- Scheme departments were most affected—"use AI to generate drawings first, then have主创 modify" became the new process
- In 2024, architecture major enrollment experienced a historic decline[^27]

**Income Changes**:

Changes in Chinese architectural designers' income:[^28]
- 2019: Scheme architects average monthly salary approximately 15,000-25,000 RMB
- 2024: Scheme architects average monthly salary dropped to approximately 10,000-18,000 RMB
- Decline of approximately 30%-40%

The situation in the U.S. is slightly better, but junior architect salary growth has明显 slowed:[^29]
- 2019-2023: Junior architect salary annual growth rate dropped from 3.5% to 1.2%

**Industry Consolidation**:

AI has accelerated the polarization of the architectural design industry:
- **Large Design Firms** (Gensler, HOK, AECOM, etc.) actively invest in AI tools, improving efficiency but reducing entry-level positions[^30]
- **Small and Medium Design Firms** face a dilemma: not investing in AI means falling behind in efficiency, investing in AI means redundant manpower
- **Freelance Architects** and small studios have increased because AI lowered the准入 threshold—but this also means more competition

**Changes in Architectural Education**:

- In 2023, multiple architecture schools incorporated AI tools into curricula[^31]
- The UK's AA School of Architecture开设 "AI and Architectural Design" courses
- Harvard GSD, MIT Architecture Department began researching AI's impact on architectural practice
- But the traditional architectural education界's attitude toward AI remains分裂: is it a "tool" or a "threat"?

## IV. Cases: Specific People and Stories

**Case 1: When AI Generates a Better Scheme**

Zhao Wei (pseudonym), 32 years old, scheme architect at a large design institute in a first-tier city, 8 years of experience.[^32]

> "In early 2024, the institute开始推 Midjourney and self-developed AI scheme tools. Our process became: the client gives the brief, AI first generates 20 conceptual schemes, the主创 picks two or three, and then we develop them. Previously, this 'conceptual scheme' phase was the part where I felt most成就感—you spend a week or two exploring massing, space, light and shadow, and finally present it to the client and see their eyes light up. Now AI finishes in minutes, and the主创 just picks and modifies."

> "My biggest feeling is not fear of being replaced—it's a 'loss of meaning.' I've been doing architectural design for eight years, and my core competency is conceptual schemes. Now this competency has become worthless. Like a chef whose proudest skill is seasoning, but now there's a machine that can automatically season, and you just need to serve the dish."

> "Our scheme group used to have 15 people; now it's 8. Those who left were all young designers."[^33]

**Case 2: Architecture Graduate's Confusion**

Lin Xiaoyu (pseudonym), 25 years old, graduated in 2024 from a 985 university's architecture program.[^34]

> "I spent five years studying architecture, one of the最长 undergraduate programs. In freshman year, I learned hand-drawing and model-making, feeling that architect was a very 'sentimental' profession. By senior year, Midjourney came out. Our professor展示 AI-generated architectural renderings in class, and the quality was better than what most students in our class could draw.那一刻 I felt five years were wasted."

Among my classmates, one-third went to real estate companies or completely changed careers."[^35]

**Case 3: AI Enables Small Studios**

Liu Hai (pseudonym), 40 years old, independent architect, founded a personal studio in 2024.[^36]

> "I worked at a large design institute for fifteen years and came out to open my own studio in 2024. Previously, this was impossible—a project required a complete team for scheme, structure, MEP, and rendering. Now with AI tools, I use Midjourney and Architechtures for schemes, Swapp for construction drawings, and Stable Diffusion for renderings. I alone plus one assistant can take on projects that previously required ten people."

> "AI is bad news for large design institutes—they win by having many people. But it's good news for independent architects like me—AI has放大个人能力 tenfold. Of course, competition has also intensified tenfold."[^37]

## V. Response: Who Adapted, Who Left

**Those Who Adapted**:

- **AI-Augmented Architects**: Integrating AI tools into workflows, improving efficiency by 3-5x, becoming "one-person design institutes"
- **Design Director Type**: Shifting from "drawing schemes" to "judging schemes"—selecting the最优解 from 50 AI-generated schemes, then赋予 soul
- **Cross-Domain Integration**: Architect + AI tool developer, designing their own parametric tools and workflows
- **Place-Based Architects**: Focusing on areas AI cannot replace—field research, community engagement, material tactility, construction coordination

**Those Who Left**:

- **Scheme Modelers**: Pure SketchUp/Rhino modeling work significantly replaced by AI
- **Rendering Artists**: Stable Diffusion and Midjourney have caused traditional rendering companies' business to暴跌[^38]
- **Construction Drawing Drafters**: Standardized construction drawings are being侵蚀 by automation tools
- **Career Transition Directions**: Product managers (tech industry), interaction design, urban data analysis, AI architecture tool development

**Fields Still Requiring Humans**:

- **Complex Project Coordination**: Large mixed-use projects involve multi-disciplinary coordination, client communication, and on-site judgment[^39]
- **Historic Building Preservation**: Requires cultural understanding and craftsmanship that AI cannot胜任
- **Extreme Environment Architecture**: Special environments like plateaus and polar regions require field experience and engineering intuition[^40]
- **Community Engagement and Humanistic Care**: Deep understanding of user needs in architectural design requires face-to-face communication[^41]

**Architectural Education Adjustments**:

Some architecture schools have begun adjusting curricula:
- Reducing hand-drawing and model-making class hours, increasing AI tool instruction
- Strengthening "design thinking" and "critical thinking" rather than "representation techniques"
- But controversy remains: is AI a "tool" or a "replacement"? Does teaching students to use AI help or harm them?

## Assessment

The architect's story is a story of **the shattering of the creativity myth.**

We have always believed that "creativity" is humanity's last bastion. Translation can be done by machines, calculation can be done by machines, but "creation"—conceiving a building from nothing—only humans can do. Architects are the perfect代言人 for this belief: they are artists wearing hard hats, using pencils and rulers to shape city skylines.

AI is puncturing this myth.

When Midjourney can generate a museum's conceptual scheme in 30 seconds—and it looks better than what most junior architects can draw—we不得不 face an uncomfortable question: **how much of "creativity" in design is actually "pattern recognition"?**

Architects don't create from a void. They learn from buildings they've seen—Le Corbusier's Notre Dame du Haut, Tadao Ando's Church of Light, Zaha's flowing curves. Their "creativity" is recombining learned patterns. And this is precisely what AI excels at.

But the architect's impact story also has an important反面—it may be **the唯一 industry where AI can "liberate" rather than "eliminate."**

The paradox of architectural design is: the most creative people are trapped in the least creative work. A talented young architect spends 80% of their time drawing lines in CAD, adjusting parameters, modifying codes—only 20% of time on真正的 "design." AI can接管 that 80% of grunt work, allowing architects to focus on 20% of creation.

This is截然不同 from financial analysts'处境. Financial analysts' "creation" (investment judgment) is difficult to quantify, and the market is unwilling to pay for it. But architects' "creation" (spatial design) has明确产出—a building, a space, an experience. The market is willing to pay for a building's "soul"—前提是 the architect can prove their soul is better than AI's.

This is the real challenge architects face: **not "will AI replace you" but "can you prove you have more soul than AI."** If you can—you will获得前所未有的 freedom. If you cannot—you will become an AI operator.

Five thousand years of architectural history tells us that great architecture is not the产物 of "pattern recognition." The Parthenon, the Great Wall, Notre Dame du Haut—they were not "generated" from existing patterns but "emerged" from humanity's deep understanding of space, light, and meaning. AI can learn all existing patterns, but it cannot yet "understand" the meaning of space to humans.

This is architects' last moat. But how long the moat can be defended, no one knows.
