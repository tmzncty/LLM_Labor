# Counselors: Chatbots Enter Therapy, but That Is Not Evidence of Replacing Licensed Care

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** The old article treated chatbot empathy as evidence of therapist replacement. By 2026 the better-supported picture is different: patients increasingly use chatbots between sessions, clinicians use AI for administrative support, but professional guidance rejects general-purpose GenAI as a substitute for qualified mental-health care. Counselor employment also remains on a high-growth trajectory.

BLS projects substance-abuse, behavioral-disorder and mental-health counselors to grow 17% from 2024 to 2034, adding about 81,000 jobs.[^1]

APA's 2026 survey of more than 1,200 licensed U.S. psychologists found that more than three-quarters said patients were discussing AI in therapy. Patients use chatbots for support, self-diagnosis, relationships and companionship.[^2]

Clinicians themselves are also adopting AI. APA's 2025 Practitioner Pulse Survey found the share of psychologists who had never used AI for work fell from 71% in 2024 to 44% in 2025; 29% used it at least monthly. Use was more common for research, writing and administrative work than clinical assistance.[^3]

APA's health advisory says general-purpose GenAI chatbots are generally not designed or validated to deliver psychotherapy and should not replace qualified providers. Specialized tools may have adjunctive value in defined contexts.[^4]

In APA's 2026 survey, 94% of psychologists said current chatbots cannot treat mental-health conditions with appropriate nuance, while 97% worried that they could reinforce negative behavior or delusional beliefs.[^5] These are professional attitudes, not randomized clinical outcome data, but they are an important governance signal.

## Evidence boundaries

- BLS 17% applies to a U.S. counselor occupation group.
- Clinician concern is not a clinical trial.
- User engagement with chatbots is not treatment efficacy.
- Evidence for specialized digital interventions should not be generalized to general-purpose chatbots.
- Text-rated “empathy” should not be equated with longitudinal therapy outcomes.
