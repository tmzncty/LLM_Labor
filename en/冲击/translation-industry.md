# Translation: From “Machine Replacement” to Post-Editing and Rate Restructuring

> **v2 audit: 2026-08-28.** Translation is among the professions most directly exposed to generative AI, but “the profession is disappearing” is still too strong. Better evidence shows lost commissions and income pressure among some freelancers, while machine-translation post-editing has become part of normal service bundles.

A 2024 Society of Authors survey of nearly 800 authors, translators and illustrators found that 36% of translator respondents said they had already lost work because of generative AI, 43% said income had fallen, and 77% expected future income harm.[^1] This was a voluntary professional survey, not a random census of all translators.

The Society again cited those results in 2025 when calling on publishers to continue commissioning human translators.[^2]

The American Translators Association's 2025 compensation survey also shows post-edit machine translation (PEMT) appearing in freelancers' service mixes.[^3] Human work is therefore not simply disappearing; it is often being redefined from translating from scratch to reviewing machine output, correcting terminology and context, and assuming final responsibility.

That redefinition matters because clients may price “post-editing” below “translation” even where the human must verify or substantially rewrite the output.

## Evidence boundaries

- The SoA 36%/43% figures are respondent shares, not global unemployment rates.
- PEMT use does not mean translators have been replaced.
- Machine-translation quality varies sharply by domain and risk.
- A post-editing contract does not automatically imply lower total earnings.
- Unsupported global extinction timelines and fabricated worker stories are removed.
