# The Translation Industry

> A profession with two thousand years of history is dying. From the translators on the Silk Road to the simultaneous interpreters at the United Nations, translation has always been a bridge for civilizational exchange. After 2023, machine translation surpassed human translators in most scenarios for the first time. Freelance translators' income shrank by 30%-50% within two years, translation companies began to close, and MTPE (Machine Translation Post-Editing) is replacing full human translation.

## I. Before the Impact: What This Profession Used to Be

Translation is one of humanity's oldest professional skills. From the translation of Buddhist scriptures to simultaneous interpretation at the United Nations, translators have always been bridges for civilizational exchange.

**Market Size**: In 2022, the global language services market was valued at approximately $65 billion.[^1] Translation services accounted for about 60% of this, with a market size of approximately $39 billion.

**Number of Practitioners**: There are approximately 640,000 professional translators worldwide, of which about 300,000 are freelancers.[^2] China has approximately 1 million translation practitioners, of which about 300,000 are freelancers.[^3]

**Income Levels**:
- U.S. freelance translators: average hourly rate of $30-50, annual income approximately $50,000-$80,000[^4]
- European freelance translators: average hourly rate of €25-40, annual income approximately €40,000-€60,000[^5]
- Chinese freelance translators: average rate of 80-150 RMB per thousand characters, annual income approximately 100,000-200,000 RMB[^6]

**Work Model**: Freelance translators receive orders through translation companies. Translation companies obtain projects from clients, assign them to translators, then review, proofread, and deliver. This is a clearly divided industrial chain.

**Professional Threshold**: Translation is not merely language conversion. Professional translation requires:
- Mastery of at least two languages
- Knowledge of specialized fields (law, medicine, technology, etc.)
- Proficiency in translation techniques (faithfulness, expressiveness, and elegance)
- Familiarity with CAT tools (Computer-Assisted Translation)

## II. The Impact: What AI Did

In November 2022, ChatGPT was released. In March 2023, GPT-4 was released. The translation industry began to feel the real impact.

**Quality Breakthrough**:

GPT-4's translation quality reached professional translator levels in most scenarios.[^7] Particularly:
- Daily conversation and business documents: nearly perfect
- Technical documentation: accurate and fluent
- Legal and medical documents: requires proofreading, but quality is already high
- Literary translation: still inferior to humans, but the gap is narrowing

**Google Translate's Evolution**:

Google Translate upgraded to the PaLM 2 model in 2023, significantly improving translation quality.[^8] Especially for minor languages, quality improved by more than 50%.

**The Rise of MTPE**:

MTPE (Machine Translation Post-Editing) became the new industry standard.[^9] The workflow became:
1. Generate initial draft using machine translation
2. Human translator reviews and revises
3. Delivery

This method is 3-5 times faster than full human translation and costs 50%-70% less.[^10]

## III. The Numbers: Layoffs/Income Decline/Industry Scale Changes

**Freelance Translator Income Changes**:

According to a survey by ProZ.com (the world's largest freelance translator platform):[^11]
- 2022: Freelance translator average income declined by 15%
- 2023: Average income declined by 30%
- 2024 (projected): Average income declined by 40%-50%

**Translation Company Closures**:

In 2023, approximately 20% of global translation companies closed or were acquired.[^12] Particularly small and medium-sized translation companies, unable to compete with AI on price.

**Job Reductions**:

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics:[^13]
- 2022: The U.S. had 75,000 translators and interpreters
- 2032 (projected): Decrease to 68,000, a reduction of 9.4%

**MTPE Replacing Full Human Translation**:

In 2023, approximately 60% of translation projects used the MTPE model, a 30% increase from 2022.[^14] Full human translation projects are becoming fewer and prices are decreasing.

## IV. Cases: Specific People and Stories

**Case 1: A Veteran Translator's Income Shrinks**

Zhang Ming (pseudonym), 35 years old, freelance English-Chinese translator, 10 years of experience.[^15]

In 2021, his annual income was approximately 250,000 RMB, primarily translating technical documents and business contracts. In 2023, his income dropped to 150,000 RMB because:
- Translation companies began using MTPE, reducing the price per thousand characters from 120 RMB to 60 RMB
- Clients began directly using GPT-4 for simple document translation
- New projects became increasingly scarce

Zhang Ming said: "I used to translate 3,000-4,000 characters per day. Now I need to translate 6,000-8,000 characters to maintain the same income. And most of my time is spent correcting machine translation errors rather than actual translation."[^16]

**Case 2: Translation Major Students Cannot Find Jobs**

Li Hua (pseudonym), 24 years old, graduated in 2023 from a university in Beijing with a translation major.[^17]

She sent out over 200 resumes, received only 5 interview invitations, and ultimately did not find translation work. She now works in administration at a foreign trade company with a salary of 5,000 RMB/month.

Li Hua said: "Our teachers told us that translation is a stable profession that will never be replaced by machines. But the year we graduated, ChatGPT came out. Of the 30 students in our class, only 3 found translation-related work."[^18]

**Case 3: Translation Company Layoffs**

Wang Qiang (pseudonym), former employee of a large translation company, laid off in 2023.[^19]

Wang Qiang said: "The company used to have over 200 employees. In 2023, it was reduced to 100 people. Mainly cutting proofreading and project management positions because the MTPE process doesn't require as much manual intervention. The company called this 'digital transformation,' but we all know AI replaced us."[^20]

## V. Response: Who Adapted, Who Left

**Successfully Transitioned to AI Translation Reviewers**:

Some translators successfully transitioned to MTPE specialists. They no longer translate but instead correct machine translation errors. This type of work has the following characteristics:
- Lower income than full human translation, but better than unemployment
- Higher workload because more text needs to be processed
- Changed skill requirements: from "translation" to "proofreading"

**Fields Still Requiring Humans**:

- Legal translation: Contracts and legal documents require extremely high accuracy; machine translation still makes errors[^21]
- Literary translation: Poetry, novels, and scripts require creativity and cultural understanding; machine translation cannot handle these[^22]
- Simultaneous interpretation: Requires real-time response and contextual understanding; machine translation still has delays and errors[^23]
- Localization: Requires understanding of target market culture and customs; machine translation lacks this understanding[^24]

**Translators Who Left the Industry**:

Many translators chose to change careers:
- Transitioning to English education (but the education industry is also being impacted by AI)
- Transitioning to foreign trade and cross-border e-commerce
- Transitioning to content creation and writing
- Completely leaving the language industry

## Assessment

Translation is a profession with two thousand years of history. From Xuanzang's translation of Buddhist scriptures to simultaneous interpretation at the United Nations, translators have always been bridges for civilizational exchange. But after 2023, this bridge is collapsing.

The rise of AI translation is not gradual—it is sudden. In 2022, machine translation was merely "usable"; in 2023, GPT-4's translation quality surpassed human translators in most scenarios. This is not quantitative change—it is qualitative change.

The困境 of the translation industry exposes a deeper problem: **the vulnerability of professional skills**.

A translator needs 5-10 years to master two languages, then another 5-10 years to become an expert in a specific field. But AI can "learn" these skills within months—not because it is smarter, but because it can process more data.

This means that any profession based on "knowledge" and "skills" is vulnerable. Doctors, lawyers, accountants, programmers—as long as your work can be digitized, AI can learn it.

But the demise of translation also reveals AI's limitations: **it cannot understand culture**.

AI can translate words, but it cannot translate meaning. It can translate "I'm breaking up with you" into Chinese, but it cannot understand the emotions, context, and cultural implications behind those words.

The value of translation is not merely language conversion, but cultural understanding. When machines can translate words, the value of translation becomes understanding culture—and this is precisely what machines cannot do.

Therefore, the question for the translation industry is not "Will AI replace humans?" but "Where is human value?" When machines can do your work, you need to find what machines cannot do.

For translators, this means: either become an AI translation reviewer or shift to fields requiring creativity and cultural understanding. Otherwise, you will be淘汰.
