## 2. EU: Leading Legislation

### AI Act (Passed 2024)

| Content | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| High-risk AI systems | AI used in recruitment and HR management must undergo compliance assessment |
| Transparency requirements | Workers have the right to know whether they are being evaluated by AI |
| Prohibitions | Bans the use of "unacceptable risk" AI systems in the workplace |
| Effective date | Full enforcement in 2026 |

### Platform Work Directive (Passed 2024)

| Content | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Presumption of employment | Presumes platform workers as "employees" unless the platform can prove otherwise |
| Algorithm transparency | Platforms must explain to workers how algorithms assign tasks, evaluate performance, and determine compensation |
| Prohibition of algorithmic dismissal | Dismissals must be made by humans |
| Health protection | Platforms must provide occupational safety and health protections |
| Effective date | Member states must transpose into domestic law before 2026 |

Source: European Parliament[^1]

## 3. USA: Fragmented and Watchful

### Federal Level

| Event | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Biden AI Executive Order (2023-10) | Requires "protecting workers from AI-driven harm," but not legally binding |
| Congressional bills | Multiple AI-related bills have failed to pass |

### State Level

| State | Bill | Content | Status |
|-------|------|---------|--------|
| California | AB5 (2020) | Restricts use of "independent contractor" classification | In effect, but Prop 22 exempts certain platforms |
| California | Prop 22 (2020) | Exempts Uber, Lyft, etc. | In effect |
| New York City | Local Law 144 (2023) | Requires bias audits for AI recruitment tools | In effect |
| Illinois | BIPA | Biometric Information Privacy Act | In effect |

Source: State government websites[^2]

## 4. China: Development First

| Domain | Regulation/Policy | Content | Source |
|--------|------------------|---------|--------|
| AI-generated content | "Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services" (2023) | Focuses on content safety; does not address labor | CAC[^3] |
| New employment forms | "Guiding Opinions on Safeguarding Labor Rights of Workers in New Employment Forms" (2021) | Encourages platforms to protect worker rights, but not enforceable | Ministry of Human Resources[^4] |
| Data security | "Data Security Law" (2021) | Focuses on data security; does not address labor | NPC[^5] |

**Characteristics:**
- Prioritizes AI development; labor protection is secondary
- Protection of workers in new employment forms relies on "guiding opinions," not enforceable rules
- Labor relationship determination for platform workers (delivery riders, ride-hailing drivers) remains disputed

## 6. International Organizations

| Organization | Stance | Specific Recommendations | Source |
|-------------|--------|-------------------------|--------|
| International Labour Organization (ILO) | Calls for platform worker protection | Include platform workers in labor protections; algorithm transparency; cross-border labor protection | ILO 2024[^7] |

## 7. Policy Gaps

| Domain | Global Status | Impact |
|--------|--------------|--------|
| Annotator mental health | Almost no protections | PTSD, anxiety, depression not recognized as work injuries |
| Algorithm transparency (Global South) | No requirements | Annotators do not know how algorithms affect their work |
| Cross-border labor protection | No framework | Annotators in Kenya, AI companies in US; no jurisdiction |
| Compensation for AI-caused unemployment | No system | Workers displaced by AI receive no compensation |
| Data annotation industry standards | No unified standards | Labor conditions vary dramatically across platforms |
