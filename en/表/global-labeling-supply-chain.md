# Global AI Data and Labeling Labor Supply Chain

This is not a complete vendor map. Client relationships change and are often confidential. It records verifiable layers and examples while marking unknowns.

| Layer | Function | Labor issue |
|---|---|---|
| Model/product firms | Define data, evaluation, safety and expert-feedback needs | Procurement standards and final responsibility may be opaque |
| Primary data vendors/platforms | Break projects into tasks, recruit, QA | Pricing, account governance, project churn |
| Regional subcontractors | Local hiring, scheduling, training, moderation | Employer identity, labor-law coverage, health responsibility |
| Crowd/expert platforms | Route prompts, ratings, rubrics and labels | Contractor status, unpaid waiting, appeals |
| Front-line workers | Label, moderate, rank, write prompts, evaluate | Pay, health, occupational identity, organizing |
| Review/QA | Correct, arbitrate and validate outputs | Human oversight itself becomes labor |

Documented examples include the historical OpenAI–Sama Kenya safety-labeling project, Scale/Outlier-style model-training markets, MTurk as an institutional ancestor of digital piecework, and newer expert-feedback markets.

Do not infer undisclosed end clients from a platform task, treat advertised rates as average earnings, or assume one vendor relationship represents an entire firm's current supply chain.
