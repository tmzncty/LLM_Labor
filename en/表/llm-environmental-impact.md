# LLM / AI Data-Centre Environmental and Infrastructure Data

Updated 2026-08-28. The most comparable global metrics are at **data-centre level**, not a timeless “energy per model query” number. AI is only part of data-centre load, and task intensity varies enormously.

| Metric | Value | Type |
|---|---:|---|
| Global data-centre electricity, 2024 | ~415 TWh | IEA 2025 estimate; ~1.5% of global electricity |
| Global data-centre electricity, 2025 | ~485 TWh | IEA 2026 updated estimate; +17% y/y |
| Global data-centre electricity, 2030 | ~950 TWh | IEA 2026 central projection; ~3% of global electricity |
| AI-focused data-centre electricity growth, 2025 | ~50% | IEA 2026 estimate |
| US electricity-demand growth attributable to data centres, 2026–2030 | ~half | IEA Electricity 2026 projection |
| Indirect data-centre electricity CO2, 2024 | ~180 Mt CO2 | All data-centre workloads, not AI alone |
| Base-case data-centre power CO2 around 2030 | ~320 Mt CO2 | Scenario projection |

IEA's 2026 update says energy use per AI task has fallen rapidly, while video generation, reasoning and agentic tasks can consume hundreds or thousands of times more energy than simple text generation. A single universal “Wh per query” figure is therefore misleading.

Data centres are geographically concentrated, so local grid, transformer, generation and storage impacts can be much larger than their global ~3% share suggests.

The buildout is also labor: electricians, pipefitters, HVAC, construction and power-equipment workers are needed during expansion, while long-run on-site staffing is much smaller than construction-phase employment.

Sources: IEA *Energy and AI* (2025), *Key Questions on Energy and AI* (2026), and *Electricity 2026*.
