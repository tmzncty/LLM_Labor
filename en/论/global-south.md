# The Global South: An Analytical Lens, Not a Single AI Labor World

“Global South” can illuminate geographic inequality in AI supply chains, but treating dozens of countries as one pool of cheap labor obscures more than it explains.

ILO's 2025 global index actually finds higher GenAI occupational exposure in richer economies: roughly 11% of employment in low-income countries versus 34% in high-income countries has some exposure.[^1] Highly digitized clerical and professional work is more common in richer economies.

A different inequality appears in training and moderation supply chains. Audited cases from Kenya, African data work, Venezuela and cross-border platforms show that parts of model training and safety work can be outsourced to labor markets with weaker bargaining power.

The key question is whether capability gains and labor costs are geographically separated—not whether “the South” is uniformly replaced by AI.

Colonialism is a strong historical concept. Use it only when specific mechanisms of control—pricing, ownership, contracts, liability or data extraction—are demonstrated.
