# Digital Labor: Distinguish Platform Use, Data Production and Paid Tasks

The old article treated “every second you scroll” as unpaid labor. A more useful framework separates different relations instead of calling all online activity labor.

Terranova's “free labor” and later digital-labor theory examine how participation, attention and user content can generate platform value. But producing value does not automatically make every online act “labor” in a legal or empirical sense.

AI-era digital labor includes at least four forms:
1. paid task work on crowd/AI-training platforms;
2. content moderation and safety work;
3. expert knowledge work that creates prompts, rubrics and evaluations;
4. user activity/data that may feed product improvement or training, where the labor status depends on the theoretical and contractual definition.

The archive therefore compares control, pay, task specification, data rights, exit, risk and bargaining power rather than simply asking whether an activity happens online.

AI training also expands digital labor upward into professional judgment, not only low-skill clicks.

References: Tiziana Terranova, “Free Labor” (2000); Christian Fuchs, *Digital Labour and Karl Marx* (2014). Empirical base: revised MTurk, Outlier, moderation and expert-training entries.
