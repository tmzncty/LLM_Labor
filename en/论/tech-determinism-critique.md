# Critique of Technological Determinism: Capability Is Not Destiny

Technological determinism turns “a model can perform a task” into “organizations and occupations must change in one predetermined way.”

ILO's 2025 global exposure work argues that job transformation is more likely than universal whole-job replacement.[^1] US Census-based 2026 evidence finds most adopting firms use AI for augmentation, while direct employment reductions are uncommon.[^2]

Meta's failed 2026 Project OT is a strong counterexample: an ambitious AI-native restructuring was scaled back because of worker resistance, disappointing productivity, reliability and security problems.[^3]

Collective agreements provide another counterexample. WGA and SAG-AFTRA rules alter which AI uses are contractually permissible, demonstrating that law and bargaining mediate technical capability.

A better causal chain is: capability → feasible task changes → adoption decisions → workflow redesign → labor outcomes. Every step is shaped by prices, institutions, regulation, error rates and worker power.

Critiquing determinism does not deny technical constraints. It rejects the move from “possible” to “inevitable.”
