# Putting AI Into the Contract: Consent, Compensation, and Control Through Collective Bargaining

> **As of 2026-08-28. Evidence status: SAG-AFTRA's 2025 and 2026 agreements are documented in union primary materials.**

One concrete way to govern AI at work is not to settle an abstract argument about whether technology is good or bad, but to write conditions of use into an enforceable labor agreement.

SAG-AFTRA continued doing this in its 2025 video-game agreement and 2026 TV/Theatrical agreement.

## The 2025 video-game agreement

SAG-AFTRA members approved the 2025 Interactive Media Agreement by 95.04% to 4.96%, concluding the related strike.[^1]

Union materials described AI provisions including:

- informed-consent requirements across multiple uses;
- disclosure and compensation rules for digital replicas;
- an ability to suspend consent for generation of new material with a digital replica during a strike;
- collectively bargained minimum compensation for certain replica and real-time-generation uses.[^2]

The agreement did not ban all AI. It turned the dispute into specific questions: who can use a performer's voice or likeness, for what purpose, for how long, with what consent, and at what price?

## The 2026 TV/Theatrical agreement

SAG-AFTRA members approved the 2026 TV/Theatrical contracts by 91.42% in June 2026. The union said the new deal strengthened earlier digital-replica protections and added guardrails against synthetic material replacing human performance.[^3]

This shows that AI terms were not merely a one-off strike settlement. They were revised again in the next major contract cycle.

## Why individual consent is not the whole issue

A worker clicking “I agree” does not necessarily have bargaining power. Collective agreements differ from platform terms because conditions are negotiated across a workforce and tied to minimum compensation, permitted uses, and strike rights.

AI labor protection is therefore not only a privacy question. It is a question of price and control.

## Why replicas change the unit of performance

Traditional performance is sold as a recording session, motion-capture session, or shoot. A digital replica can turn one capture into the input for many future generated performances.

The contract questions expand accordingly:

- may the material train a model?
- may it generate new lines?
- may it be reused in another title?
- is renewed consent required?
- is renewed compensation required?
- can generation continue during a strike?

## Limits to generalization

Actors have a strong union, identifiable rights in voice and likeness, and mature bargaining infrastructure. Many annotators, freelancers, and white-collar contractors do not.

Still, the SAG-AFTRA model offers a template: define specific uses, link consent to compensation, create withdrawal or suspension mechanisms, and put the rules in enforceable contracts rather than voluntary ethics statements.
