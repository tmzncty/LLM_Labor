# Who Trains AI: From Low-Wage Annotation to Expert Gig Work

Human-feedback labor is stratifying. Alongside moderation and basic annotation, journalists, mathematicians, physicians and PhD-level experts are being hired to turn professional judgment into prompts, rubrics, rankings and corrections.

The Reuters Institute reported in June 2026 on journalists doing AI-training work that closely resembles editorial judgment.[^1] The Guardian reported in August 2026 on Hollywood writers, directors and producers taking AI-training gigs amid weaker industry opportunities.[^2]

Outlier publicly recruits domain experts to write difficult prompts, build grading rubrics and evaluate model reasoning; advertised rates vary widely by specialty.[^3][^4]

These advertised rates are not global average earnings. The evidence supports a new stratified knowledge-gig market, not a single wage level or an inevitable replacement outcome.
