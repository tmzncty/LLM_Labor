# Who Is Being Forgotten: High Exposure, Low Adaptive Capacity

The workers most discussed are not always the most vulnerable.

A 2026 NBER study separates AI exposure from adaptive capacity. Among 37.1 million US workers in the top AI-exposure quartile, most are in occupations with above-median adaptive capacity, but about 6.1 million workers are both highly exposed and in low-adaptive-capacity occupations, concentrated in clerical and administrative work.[^1]

The EU's Quality Jobs Act consultation also highlights subcontracting-chain transparency and liability, relevant to AI supply chains where responsibility can disappear across multiple vendors.[^2]

AI infrastructure creates construction and skilled-trades work too. Reuters reported Meta's $115 million workforce program for data-center buildout, while noting that long-term on-site staffing is much smaller than construction-phase employment.[^3]

The archive should therefore prioritize workers with weak transition buffers, fragmented contractual status and hidden positions in subcontracting chains.
