# Who Regulates: AI Labor Rules Move from Principles Toward Enforcement

By 2026, workplace-AI regulation is becoming more concrete: algorithmic management, human review, excessive monitoring, transparency and responsibility chains.

The European Commission's July 2026 second-stage consultation on a Quality Jobs Act explicitly covers algorithmic management and AI at work, human-centered automated decisions, excessive monitoring, subcontracting-chain rights and skills transitions.[^1]

Some AI Act transparency obligations began applying on 2 August 2026, but many high-risk employment-related obligations apply later. “The AI Act is in force” therefore does not mean every workplace AI system is already under the full regime.[^2][^3]

OECD employer evidence across six countries shows algorithmic management is already widespread and raises concerns about accountability, explainability and worker health; worker consultation can mitigate risks.[^4]

Regulation also occurs through collective bargaining and contracts, not only statutes.
