# Who Left? A Documented Evidence Gap

> **Status: HOLD.** Public evidence is not yet strong enough to map where workers allegedly displaced by AI actually went.

The 2026 NBER adaptive-capacity study shows that many highly AI-exposed occupations contain workers with relatively strong transition capacity, while about 6.1 million workers are both highly exposed and in low-adaptive-capacity occupations, concentrated in clerical and administrative work.[^1] This measures capacity, not observed post-displacement destinations.

Danish administrative data find little average change in earnings or hours two years after ChatGPT, although some AI adopters moved into higher-paying, more AI-relevant occupations.[^2] That is evidence of some mobility, not a complete displacement map.

This article stays on HOLD until longitudinal worker-level evidence can distinguish job loss, voluntary switching, unemployment and labor-force exit—and separate AI from ordinary business cycles.
